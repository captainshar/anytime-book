# Reader test: instructions for the tester

You are testing one page of the Anytime Book, a free plain-language guide for adults entering
mainstream US life from hard starting points. You have **the page and nothing else**. Do not read
any other file in the repo: not the brief, not the style guide, not other pages. If you already
know what the editor intended, the test is void.

You are not editing. You are reporting what a reader would experience.

## Part 1: three cold reads

Read the page three times, once as each person. Stay in their situation: what they know, what
they're afraid of, what they have in their pocket.

1. **Left a high-control group last month.** Sharp and wary. Has been handled, managed, and
   recruited their whole life and notices it instantly. Little money, no family to call. Distrusts
   anything that sounds like a sermon, a sales pitch, or therapy-speak.
2. **Homeschooled and isolated, now on their own.** Bright, with gaps. Never did the ordinary
   things (a public pool, a class, a 911 call, a hobby) and doesn't know what is normal. Ashamed of
   not knowing and won't ask a stranger. May read below a high-school level.
3. **Arrived recently from a very different culture.** Reading in a second language, on a phone,
   on a break at work. US systems, brand names, and idioms are unfamiliar. Family, faith, modesty,
   or rules about men and women may matter a great deal to them.

For each reader, answer:

- **First action:** after reading, what is the first thing you would actually do? (If nothing, say so and why.)
- **Did not understand:** quote any word, phrase, or sentence you couldn't follow.
- **Talked down / scared / blocked:** quote any line that felt like being handled or cheered on, any
  line that frightened you more than it helped, and any place you got stuck (needs ID, money, a
  smartphone, a car, or a call you wouldn't make) with no other way offered.
- **Still want to know:** what you came for that the page didn't tell you.
- **Trust:** one line. Did the page seem to know people like you exist?

Keep each reader to about 150 words. Quote the page exactly.

## Part 2: the question test

You'll be given a numbered list of questions. For each one, answer **using only the page**:

- `answered`: quote the line or lines (up to 40 words) that answer it.
- `partly`: quote what's there and say what's missing.
- `not answered`: say so. Do not answer from your own knowledge.

## Part 3: the editor's five

One line each:

1. If every link on this page died tomorrow, what could a reader still do with it?
2. Which section could be cut without any reader missing it?
3. What is the most likely way this page gets someone hurt, or wastes their one free afternoon?
4. Which resource would you click first, and which would you never click?
5. One change that would help the most.

Report plainly. No praise, no summary of the page. If the page is good, the report is short.
