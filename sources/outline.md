# The Anytime Book

*A map for joining the world — at any age, from any starting point.*

## Welcome

This book is a **map**, not a test. You don’t have to read it in order. You can’t fall behind, and you can’t fail it. There is no grade at the end.

Maybe no one taught you the things in here. That is not your fault. A lot of people reach adulthood without these skills — people who were home-schooled, kept apart from others, raised in a strict group, or who came from another country or another world. The skills can still be learned. People learn them every day. So can you.

Think of this like the start of a game. The world is big and open. You can go anywhere. Some paths are easier once you’ve walked a few others first, and when that’s true, this book will tell you. But you are in charge of the order. You pick what matters to you right now.

Take what helps. Leave the rest. Come back any time.

## How to use this book

This book is split into **Areas**. Each Area holds a few smaller **Topics**.

Every Topic is written the same way, so you always know what you’re looking at:

- **What it is** — a plain description, in one or two lines.
- **Why it helps you** — what changes in your life once you have this.
- **Good to have first** — other Topics that make this one easier. (Skip this if you’re ready now.)
- **This opens up** — what gets easier *after* you have this.

A few promises:

- **Free and low-cost help exists for everything in this book.** Some Topics cost money, but many do not. Where money is needed, there are usually charities, programs, or free options too.
- **You don’t need money, an ID, or a family to begin.** A few of the very first Topics help you get those things.
- **Going slow is fine.** One small step still counts.

When you’re ready, pick an Area.

## Area 1 — First Things

These are the quiet skills that sit under everything else. If a later Topic ever feels too hard, the missing piece is often back here. There is no shame in starting at the start.

### 1.1 Reading and Writing

**What it is:** Reading words and sentences, and writing them yourself.
**Why it helps you:** Almost everything else — jobs, forms, money, the law — runs on reading. Adults can learn this, in private, for free.
**This opens up:** Every other Area in this book.

### 1.2 Everyday Math

**What it is:** The math of daily life — adding, subtracting, percentages, tips, measuring, and counting change.
**Why it helps you:** It keeps you safe with money and lets you follow recipes, schedules, and bills.
**This opens up:** Money, Cooking, Shopping Smart.

### 1.3 Time and Schedules

**What it is:** Reading a clock and a calendar, being on time, and planning your day or week.
**Why it helps you:** Appointments, jobs, and rent all run on time. This skill is the engine behind getting things done.
**This opens up:** Work, Health, and almost every appointment you’ll ever make.

### 1.4 Taking Care of Your Body Each Day

**What it is:** Daily care — washing, teeth, hair, clean clothes, and dressing for the weather.
**Why it helps you:** It keeps you healthy and helps you feel steady around other people.
**Good to have first:** Nothing.
**This opens up:** Making Friends, Doing Well at Work.

### 1.5 Sleep

**What it is:** Getting enough rest, and habits that help you fall and stay asleep.
**Why it helps you:** Sleep makes every other thing easier — your mood, your focus, your health. It is not lazy. It is fuel.
**This opens up:** Everything. Truly.

## Area 2 — Safety and Getting Started

If you are not safe, start here. These Topics cover the very first steps into the world: proving you exist, having somewhere to be, eating, and being reachable.

### 2.1 Proof That You Exist

**What it is:** The papers that show who you are — a birth certificate, a Social Security number, and a photo ID.
**Why it helps you:** Without these, it’s hard to get a job, a home, a bank account, or care. With them, doors open. If you were born at home or off the grid, there are still ways to get them.
**This opens up:** Bank Accounts, Jobs, Housing, Health Insurance.

### 2.2 A Safe Place to Sleep Tonight

**What it is:** Emergency shelter and safe housing when you have nowhere to go.
**Why it helps you:** Safety comes first. You cannot build a life while you’re in danger.
**This opens up:** Calming Your Body, and every other Topic.

### 2.3 Food Right Now

**What it is:** Free food from pantries, meals programs, and food benefits like SNAP.
**Why it helps you:** No one can think or heal while hungry. This help exists for exactly this moment.
**This opens up:** Cooking and Eating Well.

### 2.4 A Phone and an Email Address

**What it is:** A phone (even a free or low-cost one) and your own email account.
**Why it helps you:** This is how the world reaches you — jobs, doctors, benefits, friends. Your own email, kept private, is a small act of freedom.
**This opens up:** Phone Calls and Messages, Jobs, Staying Safe Online.

### 2.5 Getting Around

**What it is:** Buses, trains, rides, and other ways to travel — including from far-out rural places.
**Why it helps you:** You can’t reach a job, a clinic, or a friend you can’t get to. This is the key to the whole map.
**Good to have first:** A Phone (helpful for maps and rides).
**This opens up:** Work, Health, Friends.

## Area 3 — Your Mind and Feelings

A hard past leaves marks. That’s normal, and it can heal. This Area is about understanding what happened, calming your body, and slowly learning who *you* are — not who you were told to be.

### 3.1 Naming What Happened

**What it is:** Learning the words for what you went through — like neglect, or emotional, spiritual, or educational abuse, or C-PTSD.
**Why it helps you:** Having a name for it is a huge relief. It shows you that you are not broken, and that you are not alone.
**This opens up:** Being Kind to Yourself, Calming Your Body.

### 3.2 Calming Your Body

**What it is:** Simple ways to settle your body when fear or stress takes over.
**Why it helps you:** When your body feels safe, you can think, learn, and rest. These tools are free and you carry them everywhere.
**Good to have first:** A Safe Place to Sleep.
**This opens up:** Clear thinking for every other Topic.

### 3.3 Being Kind to Yourself

**What it is:** Trading a harsh inner voice for a kinder one, and moving from “there’s never enough” to “I can learn and grow.”
**Why it helps you:** The cruel voice in your head was put there by others. You can turn it down. This makes failure survivable, so you can keep trying.
**This opens up:** Knowing Who You Are, Resting Without Guilt.

### 3.4 Boundaries and Saying No

**What it is:** Knowing you’re allowed to say no, and learning how to do it.
**Why it helps you:** Boundaries keep you safe from people who would use you. They are the base of every healthy relationship.
**This opens up:** Healthy Relationships, Making Friends, Doing Well at Work.

### 3.5 Knowing Who You Are

**What it is:** Finding your own likes, opinions, and choices — and learning how to *decide* things for yourself.
**Why it helps you:** If others always chose for you, choosing can feel scary or even impossible. It’s a skill, and it grows. Your taste and your “yes” and “no” are yours to find.
**Good to have first:** Being Kind to Yourself.
**This opens up:** Finding What You Enjoy, every big decision ahead.

### 3.6 Finding Meaning Again

**What it is:** Rebuilding what you believe and value after leaving a strict group or faith — including how to handle holidays, right and wrong, and the grief of what you left.
**Why it helps you:** Leaving can take your whole map of the world with it. You get to build a new one, on your own terms, at your own pace.
**Good to have first:** Naming What Happened.
**This opens up:** A steadier sense of who you are.

## Area 4 — Money

Money is a tool, not a test of your worth. This Area shows how to hold it, plan with it, grow it, and spot the traps set for people who are new to it.

### 4.1 A Bank Account

**What it is:** Opening your own checking and savings account, with no one else attached.
**Why it helps you:** A safe place for your money, away from anyone who controlled it before. It’s the start of standing on your own.
**Good to have first:** Proof That You Exist.
**This opens up:** A Budget, a Paycheck, everything money-related.

### 4.2 A Budget

**What it is:** A simple plan for the money coming in and going out.
**Why it helps you:** A budget turns worry into a plan. You see what’s real, and you feel more in control.
**Good to have first:** Everyday Math, A Bank Account.
**This opens up:** Saving, Credit, Shopping Smart.

### 4.3 Your Paycheck and Taxes

**What it is:** Reading a paycheck (what’s taken out and why) and the basics of filing taxes.
**Why it helps you:** You’ll know what you really earn, avoid scary mistakes, and claim money the government may owe *you*.
**This opens up:** Budgeting with real numbers.

### 4.4 Credit and Debt

**What it is:** What a credit score is, how to build one safely, and how debt grows.
**Why it helps you:** Good credit helps you rent a home and avoid being charged more. Knowing how debt grows keeps you out of deep holes.
**Good to have first:** A Bank Account.
**This opens up:** Renting a Home, big purchases later.

### 4.5 Saving and an Emergency Fund

**What it is:** Setting aside a small cushion of money for surprises.
**Why it helps you:** A cushion means one bad week doesn’t become a disaster. Even a small one brings real calm.
**Good to have first:** A Budget.
**This opens up:** Growing Your Money.

### 4.6 Growing Your Money

**What it is:** Letting money grow over time — high-yield savings, an employer match, and simple low-cost investing for retirement.
**Why it helps you:** Money can quietly work for you while you sleep. Starting small and early beats starting big and late.
**Good to have first:** An Emergency Fund.
**This opens up:** Long-term safety and choices.

### 4.7 Money Traps to Avoid

**What it is:** Spotting traps aimed at people new to money — payday loans, rent-to-own, “be your own boss” schemes (MLMs), and scams.
**Why it helps you:** People who prey on others look for those who are new and alone. Knowing the traps keeps your money yours.
**This opens up:** Confidence with money and with people.

### 4.8 Shopping Smart

**What it is:** Comparing prices, reading reviews, handling returns, and watching subscriptions.
**Why it helps you:** The same money stretches further, and you stop paying for things you don’t use.
**This opens up:** A budget that goes further.

## Area 5 — A Home and Daily Life

A home is more than walls. This Area covers finding a place and the everyday skills that keep it running and safe.

### 5.1 Finding a Place to Rent

**What it is:** Applying for a rental, reading a lease, turning on utilities, and knowing your rights as a renter.
**Why it helps you:** A home of your own is a base for everything else. Knowing the rules keeps you from being taken advantage of.
**Good to have first:** A Bank Account, Credit and Debt.
**This opens up:** A stable place to build the rest of your life.

### 5.2 Keeping a Clean, Safe Home

**What it is:** Cleaning basics, and handling cleaning products safely (some are dangerous if mixed).
**Why it helps you:** A clean home protects your health and your peace of mind. A few safety rules prevent real harm.
**This opens up:** A healthier daily life.

### 5.3 Cooking and Eating Well

**What it is:** Planning, shopping for, and cooking simple healthy meals on a budget — plus food safety and listening to hunger.
**Why it helps you:** Cooking saves money and keeps you well. If food was scarce or strict before, this is also about a calmer, kinder relationship with eating.
**Good to have first:** Food Right Now (for this moment), Everyday Math.
**This opens up:** Better health and a fuller wallet.

### 5.4 Laundry and Clothes

**What it is:** Washing and drying clothes, sorting them, and small fixes like a loose button.
**Why it helps you:** Clean, cared-for clothes last longer and help you feel ready for the world.
**This opens up:** Doing Well at Work, Making Friends.

### 5.5 Fixing Small Things

**What it is:** Small home fixes — a clogged drain, a hung picture, finding the water shut-off.
**Why it helps you:** You save money and feel capable. You don’t have to call (and pay) someone for everything.
**This opens up:** Confidence at home.

## Area 6 — Health and Your Body

Your body and mind deserve care, even if no one cared for them before. This Area helps you start your health from zero and keep it going.

### 6.1 Starting Your Medical Life

**What it is:** Building a health record from nothing — finding a doctor, catching up on shots, and getting basic care.
**Why it helps you:** If you’ve never had regular care, this is the first step. Free and low-cost clinics exist for exactly this.
**Good to have first:** Proof That You Exist (helpful, not always required).
**This opens up:** Health Insurance, ongoing care.

### 6.2 Doctor, Dentist, and Eyes

**What it is:** Regular check-ups for your body, your teeth, and your vision.
**Why it helps you:** Small problems caught early stay small. Teeth and eyes are often skipped — and both have low-cost options.
**This opens up:** Feeling and functioning better day to day.

### 6.3 Is This an Emergency?

**What it is:** Knowing when to wait, when to see a doctor, when to go to urgent care, and when to call 911.
**Why it helps you:** It saves you money and fear, and it can save a life — yours or someone else’s.
**This opens up:** Calm, confident choices in a scary moment.

### 6.4 Medicine and Prescriptions

**What it is:** How to fill a prescription, take medicine safely, and get refills — including for mental health.
**Why it helps you:** The right medicine, taken right, helps you heal. There are programs that make it affordable.
**This opens up:** Steady health.

### 6.5 Health Insurance and Benefits

**What it is:** Getting coverage and help — Medicaid, the insurance marketplace, food benefits, and disability support.
**Why it helps you:** Coverage turns scary bills into manageable ones. Many people qualify for free help and never know it.
**Good to have first:** Offices and Paperwork (helpful).
**This opens up:** Affordable care across this whole Area.

### 6.6 Sexual and Reproductive Health

**What it is:** Clear, shame-free facts about your body, birth control, pregnancy choices, and staying safe.
**Why it helps you:** Real information keeps you healthy and in charge of your own body and future.
**This opens up:** Dating, Sex, and Consent; Having Kids — or Not.

### 6.7 Alcohol, Drugs, and Other Risks

**What it is:** Honest facts about alcohol and drugs, what “normal” use looks like, and where to get help.
**Why it helps you:** Knowing the facts helps you make safe choices and recognize when you or someone you love needs support.
**This opens up:** Safer choices and steadier health.

## Area 7 — Talking to People and the World

So much of adult life is reaching out — to offices, to systems, and to people. If you were kept apart from others, this can feel like the hardest Area. Go gently. These are skills, and skills grow with practice.

### 7.1 Phone Calls and Messages

**What it is:** Making a phone call, leaving a voicemail, and writing a clear message or email.
**Why it helps you:** This one skill unlocks doctors, jobs, benefits, and friends. If phone calls scare you, you are very much not alone — and it gets easier.
**Good to have first:** A Phone and an Email Address.
**This opens up:** Offices and Paperwork, Jobs, Friends.

### 7.2 Offices and Paperwork

**What it is:** Filling out forms, dealing with offices (like the DMV or benefits offices), and what to do when you’re told “no.”
**Why it helps you:** Almost every Topic in this book passes through some office. Knowing how to handle them — and how to appeal — is real power.
**Good to have first:** Phone Calls and Messages.
**This opens up:** ID, Benefits, Housing, Taxes — nearly everything.

### 7.3 Making Friends

**What it is:** Meeting people, small talk, and slowly building real friendships.
**Why it helps you:** Friends make life lighter and safer. Being alone is hard on the body and mind; connection helps you heal.
**Good to have first:** Boundaries and Saying No.
**This opens up:** A support network, more joy.

### 7.4 Healthy Relationships and Red Flags

**What it is:** What healthy love and friendship look like, and how to spot warning signs like control or manipulation.
**Why it helps you:** If you grew up around harm, the harmful can feel normal. This helps you choose people who are safe and kind.
**Good to have first:** Boundaries and Saying No.
**This opens up:** Dating, safer relationships of every kind.

### 7.5 Dating, Sex, and Consent

**What it is:** How dating works today, what consent means, and how to keep yourself safe and respected.
**Why it helps you:** You deserve relationships built on respect and choice. Clear basics protect your body and your heart.
**Good to have first:** Healthy Relationships and Red Flags.
**This opens up:** Confident, safe connection.

### 7.6 Everyday Culture

**What it is:** The unwritten rules “everyone seems to know” — how restaurants, tipping, and small social moments work, plus a little pop culture to feel in the loop.
**Why it helps you:** Knowing the everyday rules helps you relax and belong. You’re learning a new culture, and that’s a normal, doable thing.
**This opens up:** Comfort in public, easier friendships and work.

## Area 8 — Learning and Work

Work brings money, structure, and pride. Learning opens doors that may have been locked for you. This Area covers both.

### 8.1 Computer and Internet Basics

**What it is:** Using a computer and phone — typing, searching, saving files, and getting around the internet.
**Why it helps you:** These skills are now required for jobs, school, and services. The good news: they’re very learnable.
**This opens up:** Online safety, school, and most jobs.

### 8.2 Staying Safe Online

**What it is:** Strong passwords, privacy settings, spotting fake or misleading content, and keeping your accounts safe.
**Why it helps you:** It protects your money, your identity, and your peace — and helps you keep family or others from tracking you.
**Good to have first:** Computer and Internet Basics.
**This opens up:** Safe use of everything online.

### 8.3 Your GED or Diploma

**What it is:** Earning a high school equivalency (GED) or diploma as an adult.
**Why it helps you:** It opens jobs and college. Free study tools and classes exist, and you can go at your own pace.
**Good to have first:** Reading and Writing, Everyday Math.
**This opens up:** College, better jobs.

### 8.4 Going to College

**What it is:** Applying to college and getting financial aid — including how to get aid *without* a parent’s help or signature.
**Why it helps you:** College can change your path. And there are special rules to get aid if your family won’t or shouldn’t be involved.
**Good to have first:** Your GED or Diploma.
**This opens up:** New careers and communities.

### 8.5 Finding a Job

**What it is:** Searching for work, writing a résumé, interviewing, and finding references when you don’t have any yet.
**Why it helps you:** A job brings money and footing. There are ways to start even with no work history and no contacts.
**Good to have first:** A Phone and an Email Address.
**This opens up:** Income, independence, Doing Well at Work.

### 8.6 Doing Well at Work

**What it is:** Workplace basics — how to speak and dress, getting along, your rights, using helpful tools (including AI), and what to do if you’re let go.
**Why it helps you:** Keeping and growing in a job builds your money and your confidence. Knowing your rights keeps you protected.
**Good to have first:** Finding a Job.
**This opens up:** Raises, stability, a career.

## Area 9 — Big Life Decisions

Some Topics are bigger, or come later. You don’t need these on day one. But when the time comes, here’s the map.

### 9.1 Knowing Your Rights

**What it is:** Your basic rights — with police, landlords, employers, and doctors — and how to find free legal help.
**Why it helps you:** People who know their rights are harder to push around. Free legal aid exists when you need it.
**This opens up:** Standing up for yourself safely.

### 9.2 Leaving Family Safely

**What it is:** Stepping away from a harmful family — going no-contact, changing your name, keeping your address private, and protective orders.
**Why it helps you:** If your family is a danger, you have the right to be safe and hard to find. There are careful, legal ways to do it.
**Good to have first:** Knowing Your Rights.
**This opens up:** A safer, freer life.

### 9.3 Staying Safe from People Who Prey on You

**What it is:** Spotting people who target the new and alone — for money, for labor, or worse — and learning who to trust.
**Why it helps you:** Survivors are often taught to obey, which predators look for. Learning to read people protects you.
**This opens up:** Safer relationships and choices everywhere.

### 9.4 Buying and Running a Car

**What it is:** Getting a license, buying a used car safely, insurance, and basic upkeep like oil and tires.
**Why it helps you:** A car can mean a job, freedom, and reach — especially far from a city. Knowing the basics keeps you safe and saves money.
**Good to have first:** A Budget.
**This opens up:** More jobs, more places, more freedom.

### 9.5 Having Kids — or Not

**What it is:** Planning whether and when to have children, and parenting in a kinder way than you may have known.
**Why it helps you:** This is one of life’s biggest choices, and it’s fully yours. If you do parent, you can break the cycle.
**Good to have first:** Sexual and Reproductive Health.
**This opens up:** A family on your own terms.

### 9.6 Aging, Death, and Wills

**What it is:** The plain facts about wills, what happens when someone dies, and caring for aging loved ones.
**Why it helps you:** Planning ahead spares you and the people you love from confusion and stress later.
**This opens up:** Peace of mind.

## Area 10 — Fun, Rest, and Being You

This is not the leftover Area. This is the *point*. A life isn’t only chores and safety. It’s also joy, rest, and discovering what you love. If you were never allowed these, learning them is some of the most important work there is.

### 10.1 Finding What You Enjoy

**What it is:** Trying hobbies, play, and new things to learn what *you* like.
**Why it helps you:** Joy is not a reward you have to earn. Finding what lights you up is how you build a self worth living with.
**Good to have first:** Knowing Who You Are.
**This opens up:** A richer life and new friends.

### 10.2 Resting Without Guilt

**What it is:** Learning that rest is allowed — that you’re allowed to exist without always being useful.
**Why it helps you:** Many survivors were only valued for what they produced. Rest heals your body and your worth. You don’t have to earn it.
**This opens up:** Sleep, calm, and a kinder life.

### 10.3 Swimming and Water Safety

**What it is:** Learning to swim and be safe around water, at any age.
**Why it helps you:** It’s a life-saving skill, and it can become a real joy. Adult lessons are common and low-cost.
**This opens up:** Confidence and fun.

### 10.4 Protecting Yourself

**What it is:** Basic first aid and simple self-defense.
**Why it helps you:** Knowing what to do in an emergency, and feeling able to protect yourself, builds deep, steady confidence.
**This opens up:** Safety and self-trust.

## A last word

You don’t have to do this all at once. You don’t have to do it perfectly. Every person who feels at home in the world started somewhere, and many started later than they wished.

Pick one Topic. Just one. Take one small step toward it this week.

That’s how the whole map gets walked — one step at a time.

You’re welcome here. Come back whenever you need the map.
