## Sharon should check

### 5.1 Finding a Place to Rent

- **HUD's tenant-rights-by-state pages seem to be gone.** `hud.gov/topics/rental_assistance/tenantrights` loads with a title and no content. The state pages (`hud.gov/states/<state>/renting/tenantrights`) return 404. Other HUD pages (healthy homes, the fair-housing page) show the same "25red-"/"26red-" placeholder titles, so HUD looks to be partway through a site move. USA.gov still links to the tenant-rights page (USA.gov updated April 2026). I used LawHelp.org plus the LSC legal-aid finder instead. Worth rechecking HUD in a month.
- **Legal statements to look over.** The page says (a) "in most cases" federal law bans discrimination on the 7 protected classes (I added "in most cases" because the Fair Housing Act has exemptions, like some small owner-occupied buildings); and (b) "in general, a landlord has to go through the courts to make you leave," and readers should call legal aid if they're locked out or their utilities are cut off. These are widely true, but a housing lawyer or legal aid advocate should sign off.
- **Housing counselor cost.** HUD says counseling is "free or low cost," and CFPB says "often at little or no cost." The page says "Free or low cost. Ask about fees." Gemini said "Free."
- **"If you only do one thing first"** points to the housing counselor line, not 211. Both Gemini runs agreed. Switch to 211 if you'd rather the first step be about money for move-in costs.
- The tenant-union item (from your brief) is covered only by "local legal aid offices can also tell you about tenant groups." I didn't publish a tenant-union directory (see Could not verify).

### 5.2 Keeping a Clean, Safe Home

- **Poison Help, 24/7.** The HRSA About Us page (read in a browser because automated fetch was blocked) confirms 1-800-222-1222, free, confidential, 161 languages, and that a nurse, pharmacist, or doctor answers. It does not say "24 hours" on that page. Search snippets from poisonhelp.hrsa.gov do say 24/7. So the page doesn't claim 24/7. Please confirm on the site, then add "any time, day or night" if you like.
- **National Capital Poison Center (poison.org)** closed its own call center on March 31, 2025 and now runs online only. I list its online tool and the HRSA phone line separately, so readers won't think poison.org answers calls.
- **Red Cross smoke alarms.** The page says "Availability depends on your location." I did not publish Gemini's claims of "no ID, no eligibility rules" or the 1-800-RED-CROSS number, because the page doesn't confirm them. I added "If you rent, let your landlord know first" (my judgment).
- **NPIC email.** It appears two ways (npic@oregonstate.edu and npic@ace.orst.edu), so I left email off the page. The hours have no time zone. It is Oregon State, so probably Pacific time.
- **Safety advice in "Watch out for"** (bleach mixes, foggers, smoke-alarm batteries) follows poison.org's chlorine-gas article and USFA. A quick read for tone would help.

### 5.3 Cooking and Eating Well

- **MyPlate is effectively gone.** myplate.gov now shows only a banner pointing to the new Dietary Guidelines at RealFood.gov. MyPlate Kitchen redirects there too. Gemini's "myplate.food" links are a third-party copy. I didn't list RealFood.gov. The new guidelines are a policy change, and you'll want to decide whether the book points to them. Your call.
- **SNAP-Ed.** No SNAP-Ed classes are listed. The Extension entry tells readers to *ask* what classes exist now, and says "Some free classes have ended because of funding cuts." Spend Smart. Eat Smart. (Iowa State) was historically SNAP-Ed funded, but it is still posting (Sept 2026 blog posts) and shows no notice. Recheck it next round.
- **NEDA.** It isn't listed as a resource. "Watch out for" has one line saying the NEDA helpline closed in 2023, so readers who find the old number know to use the Alliance or ANAD instead. Remove it if you'd rather not name NEDA.
- **Gentle framing.** I added a short intro paragraph under "If food feels hard in a different way" (hiding food, forgetting to eat, eating past full as a normal response, not a failing). It says the helplines don't replace treatment. Please read for tone.
- **Ellyn Satter Institute.** Held back. It has free articles on "eating competence" and the "Division of Responsibility," which suits readers from strict-food homes. But the site sells books and trainings and is built on one author's model. Include it?
- **Food-safety numbers** (2-hour rule, 3–4 days for leftovers) are standard USDA guidance. The FSIS leftovers page blocked automated reads, so I checked the numbers against the FoodSafety.gov chart (which shows 3–4 days for prepared salads). Please glance at fsis.usda.gov "Leftovers and Food Safety."
- **Good and Cheap** needs a newsletter sign-up (email) to get the free PDF. Gemini said no sign-up was needed. The page says so plainly.
- **988** is listed so there is an after-hours option. Both eating-disorder lines are weekday only.

### 5.4 Laundry and Clothes

- **Laundry Love and church hosts.** Many sites are run by churches. For readers leaving high-control religion, I added: "Laundry Love doesn't list any religious rules for getting help. If anyone pressures you, you can say no or leave." Please check the tone. The location map footer says ©2023. Spot-check a listing or two near you if you can.
- **American Cleaning Institute** is the industry trade group. I said so on the page, and added that "you don't need to buy any special brand." Swap it for a university Extension laundry guide if you'd rather avoid industry sources.
- **Dress for Success.** Each affiliate sets its own referral rules. Many are referral-only, and some take self-referrals. The page tells readers to ask, and to get a referral from a job center, shelter, or caseworker. It serves women only. There is no verified national option for men (see Hand to Gemini).
- **Laundromat card holds** are in "Watch out for." Gemini's source was a Reddit post, so I kept the wording general ("some machines put a hold"). Keep it or cut it?

### 5.5 Fixing Small Things

- **Mercury Stardust** is a trans creator who focuses on renters and is known for a trauma-informed, kind approach. I think she suits this book well. Flagging only because some readers' former communities may react, so it's a values call. Keep?
- **See Jane Drill** pushes a paid course. The page says "you don't need it."
- **Safety lines.** "If you smell gas, leave first, then call your gas company or 911 from outside" is standard utility advice, but I wrote it myself. It isn't from a checked page. Please confirm you're happy with it.
- I used a `:::tip` aside at the top: renters, ask your landlord before repairs.

### 2. Tenant unions and local tenant-rights groups (5.1)

> Topic 5.1 follow-up: How a first-time renter finds a local tenant union, tenant-rights group, or free tenant hotline in their city or state. HUD's state-by-state "Tenant Rights, Laws and Protections" pages appear to be offline as of October 2026. For each, link to the organization's own website, not Wikipedia, and say whether it is still active in 2026. Do not make state-by-state phone tables. Explain how to find the local version.

### 3. Renter-readiness classes and landlord guarantee programs (5.1)

> Topic 5.1 follow-up: Programs that help people with no rental history or no credit get approved for a first apartment. Examples include renter-education certificate classes (such as "Rent Well" or "Ready to Rent") and landlord risk-mitigation or guarantee funds. Most of these are local. List which states or cities each serves, who runs it, the cost, and whether it is active in 2026. Also say whether any national body (HUD housing counseling, NeighborWorks, Cooperative Extension) offers a renter-readiness course that is free and available online to anyone. Do not call a local program national.

### 4. Beginner-friendly cooking classes after SNAP-Ed (5.3)

> Topic 5.3 follow-up: SNAP-Ed federal funding ended after fiscal year 2025 under H.R. 1 (the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act"). Find which free, beginner cooking-on-a-budget programs are still running in 2026 without SNAP-Ed. Look at EFNEP (Expanded Food and Nutrition Education Program), Cooperative Extension, public libraries, food banks (for example, Cooking Matters), and online video courses. For each, confirm it is active in 2026 on its own site, say who funds it, and say whether it is national or local. Also note any national program that ended in 2025–2026 so the book doesn't send readers to it.

### 5. Gentle food-relationship resources for people from scarce or strict-food backgrounds (5.3)

> Topic 5.3 follow-up: Free, trauma-informed resources for adults whose relationship with food was shaped by scarcity, food used as punishment, or strict religious or group food rules (including fasting rules in high-control groups). Find non-commercial or low-cost reading, videos, or support groups that teach regular eating, hunger and fullness cues, and food security without diet-culture messages. Prefer registered dietitians, university Extension, or established eating-disorder nonprofits. Clearly label which are self-help and which are treatment. Do not include the NEDA helpline, which closed in 2023.
