Drafted 2026-10-04 by Claude. All six Topic pages are `status: draft`. This Area is high-stakes, covering legal and physical safety. Every item marked **[advocate]** or **[lawyer]** should be read by a DV advocate or a lawyer before the page is promoted to `verified`.

## Sharon should check

### 9.1 Knowing Your Rights

- **[lawyer] Police encounter bullets.** These paraphrase the ACLU "Stopped by Police" page (updated June 8, 2026): stay silent and say so out loud, some states require your name, ask "Am I free to go?", don't run, resist, or lie, and ask for a lawyer if arrested. Stop-and-identify laws differ by state. Check that the short version isn't misleading.
- **[lawyer] Civil vs. criminal paragraph.** It says you have a right to a public defender "if you could go to jail and can't pay." That is right in general. A lawyer should confirm the wording.
- **DOL immigration statement.** The page quotes WHD ("enforces the law without regard to a worker's immigration status"). Because of 2025–26 immigration enforcement, Sharon may want an immigration advocate to say whether the book should add a caution about contacting any federal agency. It appears on both 9.1 and 9.3.
- **EMTALA.** "Ask about financial assistance or charity care" is general advice and not from the CMS page. It is low risk.
- **"Speak up for change" section (new, from community input).** It is a values and tone choice to put advocacy in this Topic. The line "state lawmakers make most laws about schools, homeschooling, and family courts" is general knowledge and has no citation.

### 9.2 Leaving Family Safely: highest risk in the Area

- **[advocate] The whole page.** It is safety-critical. I left out all of Gemini's tactical advice on purpose: the "false trail" calls, burner phones, escape-route rehearsal, and the go-bag lists. The page only says "make a plan with an advocate" and links the Hotline's own safety plan tool. An advocate should decide whether to add any practical steps.
- **[advocate] Hotline and family abuse.** The page says "you can call about abuse from family, too." The Hotline's site frames its work mostly around relationship abuse. Please confirm with the Hotline or an advocate that this wording is fair.
- **[advocate/lawyer] Welfare checks and missing-person reports.** Sourced to LAPD and Las Vegas Metro PD FAQ pages, plus Phoenix PD. Each says police tell the family the adult is safe but don't share the person's location without consent. LVMPD adds "unless the person requesting has court-appointed guardianship." I wrote it as "police departments such as…" and added the guardianship caveat. It is **not a national law**. Some survivors' parents seek guardianship over disabled adult children, so this needs a lawyer's eye. The script "you can calmly say you are safe and don't want your location shared" is my wording.
- **[lawyer] Protective orders.** "In many states, family members count" and "rules and costs differ." Gemini said they are free everywhere by law. I did not publish that because I didn't verify it for all states.
- **[lawyer] Name changes.** Says fee waivers exist in many states, and that many states let you keep the case private or skip newspaper publication if you are in danger. This is general and on purpose. Points to LawHelp.org and the USAGov name-change page.
- **[lawyer] Immigrant survivors.** I mention "special protections" without naming VAWA self-petitions, T/U visas, or fee rules. Gemini's claim of a $0 VAWA fee was not verified against the current USCIS fee schedule. An immigration lawyer should decide how much to say.
- **[advocate] Address Confidentiality Programs.** The explanation is plain and has no state table. It says eligibility "most often" covers DV, sexual assault, stalking, and trafficking, and that family abuse "often counts, but check." The Missouri SOS directory shows Arkansas covers only driver's license records.
- **[advocate] Untangling from accounts.** Covers the FCC Safe Connections Act, the HIPAA confidential-communications request, credit freezes, data brokers, and device checks. The USPS Move Validation letter to the old address is in "Watch out," which tells readers to talk with an advocate about mail. Should the book suggest an alternative, such as a PO box or the ACP address? I didn't, because a PO box application asks for a physical address.
- **The FCC page has a typo.** It prints the Hotline as 1-888-799-SAFE. The book uses the correct 1-800-799-7233. Sharon could report this to the FCC.
- **Child abuse reporting (new, from community input).** Childhelp's own site says it is "not a reporting line." The page says that clearly, then links the official state reporting-number list. Please check the framing for readers worried about younger siblings. Also consider whether to tell readers they can usually report without giving their name. I didn't verify that per state, so I left it out.
- **Footsteps** is labeled NYC-based with many programs open nationwide. Recovering from Religion therapists charge fees, and the page says so.

### 9.3 Staying Safe from People Who Prey on You

- **Documents: Gemini told readers both to hide them and to keep them on their person.** I resolved it as: "Keep the originals with you or with someone you fully trust. Keep a photo or copy somewhere safe." The Trafficking Hotline's own tips say to keep documents "in your possession at all times." **[advocate]** Check whether a person still living with a controlling family should be told differently.
- **The red-flag list** is my plain rewrite. None of Gemini's "trust calibration," "hysteresis," or "safe-exit timer" framing is used, because it came from AI papers and an affair blog.
- **[advocate] Stalkerware line.** "Some people get more dangerous when they find their tracking app was removed" paraphrases techsafety.org.
- **The Trafficking Hotline is now run by Compass Connections, not Polaris.** The page doesn't name the operator.
- **"If you only do one thing first"** asks readers to keep their own ID, phone, and money in their hands. Check the tone. It shouldn't feel like blame for someone whose things were already taken.

### 9.4 Buying and Running a Car

- **The adult licensing paragraph** says "in many states" adults can go permit → practice → road test, and gives Texas (ages 18–24 need a 6-hour course) as an example. Gemini's claim that adults are "generally exempt" is not true everywhere.
- **The "free car" warning** says there is no government free-car program and real charities have long waits. It sends readers to 211. This is a judgment call.
- **California Low Cost Auto Insurance** is the only state program on the page, labeled "California only." mylowcostauto.com rendered blank on 2026-10-04, so it was verified through the CA Department of Insurance page.
- **Car Care Council.** I couldn't confirm who funds it (its about page returned 404), so the page makes no claim about funding.

### 9.5 Having Kids — or Not

- **[medical/legal] Abortion law changes.** The page doesn't state any law. It points to Charley and the M+A Hotline, and says laws differ by state and have changed. - **Harmful parenting books.** I didn't name *To Train Up a Child* or *Babywise*, because Gemini's sources were blogs and Wikipedia. Sharon can decide whether to name them after finding an AAP or medical source.
- **Childhelp for a parent at their limit.** Childhelp's stated audience is "anyone concerned about or affected by child abuse." Please confirm it fits. The "put the baby down safely and step away" line is standard shaken-baby prevention advice and is not from Childhelp's page.

### 9.6 Aging, Death, and Wills

- **[lawyer] Default decision-makers and intestacy.** "State law usually picks your closest relatives" is general and correct, but the order differs by state.

### 1. Police welfare checks and missing-adult reports (9.2)

> Topic: When a family reports an adult as missing or asks police for a "welfare check." For adults (18+) who have left a controlling or abusive family, find official sources (state laws, state police or attorney general pages, large city police department policies, legal aid guides, DV coalitions) that explain: whether an adult has to talk to police, whether police will tell the family where the adult is, what the adult can say to officers, how an adult can tell police ahead of time that they left on purpose and are safe, and what changes if a family member holds guardianship or a power of attorney. Note any states with written rules. Cite each claim to an official page. Flag anything that differs by state. Include how DV advocates or legal aid can help, and warn about family members who try to get guardianship to regain control.

### 2. Candidate new Topic: Helping someone still inside (siblings, friends, or a partner still in the family or group)

> Topic: How an adult who has left an abusive family, isolating homeschool, or high-control group can safely help someone still inside, such as younger siblings, a parent, or friends. Find: how and when to report suspected child abuse, neglect, or educational neglect (official state CPS reporting, the Child Welfare Information Gateway, and what happens after a report); how to keep contact safely without putting the person at risk; what support exists for siblings who age out at 18 (shelters, youth programs, FAFSA independence); advocacy groups working on homeschool oversight, such as the Coalition for Responsible Home Education, with their current website and status; and how to contact state legislators. Include legal limits, like what an adult sibling can and can't do, and how to protect your own safety and address while helping.

### 3. Learning to drive as an adult with no one to teach you (9.4)

> Topic: Getting a first driver's license as an adult (18+) with no family to teach you and no car. Find: how adult permit and road-test rules differ by state (which states require adult driver ed, and for which ages); how to find state-licensed driving schools and adult courses, with typical costs; free or low-cost driving lessons (community colleges, workforce programs, refugee and newcomer programs, nonprofits); programs that lend a car for the road test; and help paying license fees. Give the official state finder for each item rather than a state table. Warn about unlicensed instructors and fake "guaranteed license" services.

### 4. Low-income car ownership and low-cost insurance (9.4)

> Topic: Real low-income car ownership and insurance programs in 2026. Confirm whether the National Consumer Law Center's "Working Cars for Working Families" directory is still online and maintained. List established nonprofit car-ownership programs (low-cost cars, fair car loans, matched savings, car repair grants) with the states they serve. List every state-run low-cost auto insurance program (for example California's program, New Jersey's Special Automobile Insurance Policy, Hawaii's plan), with official state pages, eligibility, and what they don't cover. Note TANF or workforce car-repair help and how to ask for it. Flag charities with privacy risks or requirements to promote them on social media.

### 5. Free parenting support for parents without a family "village" (9.5)

> Topic: Free support for new parents who had harsh or neglectful childhoods. Find: official finders for evidence-based home visiting programs (HRSA's MIECHV and state home visiting finders, Healthy Families America, Parents as Teachers, Nurse-Family Partnership/Changent), with the current finder URL for each; a working national parent helpline (check whether the National Parent & Youth Helpline at 1-855-427-2736 is still running and what its official website is); free parenting classes through libraries, health departments, or WIC; and free, evidence-based guidance on non-physical discipline from the American Academy of Pediatrics or the CDC. Include AAP or medical-body statements, if any, on harsh "training" methods popular in some religious homeschool communities.

### 6. What to do when someone dies: the practical steps (9.6)

> Topic: The practical steps after a death, for someone with no family help and little money. Find official guidance on: getting a death certificate and who can order one; telling Social Security and other agencies; what a "small estate" affidavit is and how to find your state's form and court self-help page; low-cost options when no one can pay for burial or cremation (county indigent burial programs, body donation, direct cremation, VA burial benefits, crime victim compensation funeral help); and what to do if an estranged relative dies and you are listed as next of kin. Also: free or low-cost will help (legal aid, law school clinics, state bar programs), how valid online wills are by state, and any state (such as Louisiana) where common online will tools don't work.

### 7. Safe, confidential name changes (9.2)

> Topic: Changing your legal name when you are hiding from an abusive family. Find, for the US as a whole and with an official state finder: court fee waivers for name changes; states that let survivors seal the record or skip newspaper publication, and how to ask; fingerprint or background-check steps; how to update Social Security, passport, and state ID afterward; and free legal help (legal aid, trans legal services such as Advocates for Trans Equality or state groups, with current program pages). Warn about paid name-change websites.
