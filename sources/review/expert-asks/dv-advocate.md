# Review sheet: domestic violence advocate

Read [what we are asking](reviewer-brief.md) first. Pages are in order of importance. Stop whenever your time runs out.

Our readers include adults leaving abusive or controlling **families** and high-control groups, not only partners. Many still live with the people who control them, and some are watched closely.

## Start here (about 45 minutes)

### 1. Leaving Family Safely (the whole page)

[anytimebook.org/big-life-decisions/leaving-family-safely](https://anytimebook.org/big-life-decisions/leaving-family-safely/)

This is the highest-risk page in the book. We left out all tactical escape advice on purpose (false trails, second phones, go-bag lists, rehearsing a route). The page says to make a plan with an advocate and links The Hotline's safety planning tool.

- Is leaving the tactics out the right call? Is there any practical step that is safe to print for everyone?
- The page says readers can call The Hotline about abuse from family, not only from a partner. Is that fair to say?
- **Welfare checks.** The page says that when family reports an adult missing, police generally confirm the adult is safe without sharing where they are, and that a guardianship changes this. We based it on three city police departments, not a national rule. Does it match what you see? Is the suggested wording for talking to officers safe?
- **Address Confidentiality Programs.** Is the explanation right, and is "family abuse often counts, but check" a fair summary?
- **Untangling from family accounts** (phone plan, health insurance mail, credit, mail forwarding). Is anything here likely to tip off a family member? The post office mails a notice to the old address when someone files a change of address. We tell readers to talk with an advocate about mail. Should we suggest something specific?
- **If a child at home is not safe.** Is the framing right for a reader worried about younger siblings?

### 2. A Phone and an Email Address: the caution box

[anytimebook.org/safety-and-getting-started/a-phone-and-an-email-address](https://anytimebook.org/safety-and-getting-started/a-phone-and-an-email-address/), sections "If someone controls your phone plan" and "Keep your phone and email private"

- The box "Think before you turn things off" says the person tracking may notice when location sharing stops, and suggests talking to an advocate first or using a different phone. Is that the right advice, in the right order?
- The phone-plan section explains the Safe Connections Act and gives the reader words to say to the carrier. Is the script right? Does asking for a line separation ever alert the account holder in a way we should warn about?

### 3. Staying Safe Online: the tracking section

[anytimebook.org/learning-and-work/staying-safe-online](https://anytimebook.org/learning-and-work/staying-safe-online/), section "If someone may be watching or tracking you"

- We say the other person may notice changes made with Apple's Safety Check, to talk to The Hotline first if not yet safe, to save screenshots before deleting anything, and to use a safer device. Anything wrong, missing, or in the wrong order?

## If you have more time

### 4. A Safe Place to Sleep Tonight

[anytimebook.org/safety-and-getting-started/a-safe-place-to-sleep-tonight](https://anytimebook.org/safety-and-getting-started/a-safe-place-to-sleep-tonight/), section "If someone is hurting you or controlling you" and "Watch out for"

- The page says no ID or money is needed to call any of the lines listed. True in your experience?
- "Watch out for" suggests a library computer or a friend's phone if someone checks yours. Safe to say?

### 5. Staying Safe from People Who Prey on You

[anytimebook.org/big-life-decisions/staying-safe-from-people-who-prey-on-you](https://anytimebook.org/big-life-decisions/staying-safe-from-people-who-prey-on-you/)

- We tell readers to keep their ID and papers with them or with someone they fully trust, and a copy somewhere safe. Should a person still living with a controlling family be told something different?
- We say some people become more dangerous when they find a tracking app was removed. Is that worded responsibly?
- The first step asks readers to keep their own ID, phone, and money in their own hands. Does that read as blame to someone whose things were already taken?

### 6. Credit and Debt: when it's family

[anytimebook.org/money/credit-and-debt](https://anytimebook.org/money/credit-and-debt/), section "If someone used your name or number"

- We say a reader can freeze their credit first, with no police report, and then talk to legal aid about next steps when the person who used their identity is a parent. Could a freeze alert the family member? Is the order right?

### 7. Healthy Relationships and Red Flags

[anytimebook.org/talking-to-people-and-the-world/healthy-relationships-and-red-flags](https://anytimebook.org/talking-to-people-and-the-world/healthy-relationships-and-red-flags/)

- Whole page, for anything that could be read as blaming the reader or that misdescribes what a hotline call is like.

### 8. Protecting Yourself: the self-defense section

[anytimebook.org/fun-rest-and-being-you/protecting-yourself](https://anytimebook.org/fun-rest-and-being-you/protecting-yourself/), section "Learn to protect yourself"

- It covers what a good class looks like and when to walk out, with no techniques. Is the "Watch out for" list right? Options for men who are survivors are thin; do you know a good one?
