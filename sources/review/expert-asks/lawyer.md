# Review sheet: lawyer or legal aid advocate

Read [what we are asking](reviewer-brief.md) first. Pages are in order of importance. Stop whenever your time runs out.

The book is national, so every legal statement is general on purpose. We are **not** asking you to confirm the law in 50 states. We are asking: is any general statement wrong, or so simplified that a reader who relied on it could be hurt? Where should we add "this depends on your state"?

## Start here (about 45 minutes)

### 1. Leaving Family Safely: the legal parts

[anytimebook.org/big-life-decisions/leaving-family-safely](https://anytimebook.org/big-life-decisions/leaving-family-safely/), sections "Legal protection" and "Keeping your new address private"

- **Welfare checks and missing-person reports.** The page says police generally tell a family that an adult is safe without sharing the adult's location, and that this changes if someone holds a guardianship. Our sources are three city police departments. Is it a fair general statement? Some of our readers are disabled adults whose parents may seek guardianship to regain control. What should the page say about that risk, and who should a reader call?
- **Protective orders.** We say that in many states family members count, and that rules and costs differ. We did not publish a claim that orders are free everywhere. Is the wording right?
- **Name changes.** We say fee waivers exist in many states, and that many states let a person in danger keep the case private or skip newspaper publication. Fair?

### 2. Knowing Your Rights: police and courts

[anytimebook.org/big-life-decisions/knowing-your-rights](https://anytimebook.org/big-life-decisions/knowing-your-rights/), section "If the police stop you" and the note near the top

- The police bullets paraphrase the ACLU's guidance: you can stay silent and should say so out loud, some states require your name, ask if you are free to go, don't run, resist, or lie, ask for a lawyer if arrested. Is the short version misleading anywhere?
- We say a person has a right to a public defender if they could go to jail and can't pay, and that this does not cover civil cases. Is that wording safe?

### 3. Finding a Place to Rent: renter rights

[anytimebook.org/a-home-and-daily-life/finding-a-place-to-rent](https://anytimebook.org/a-home-and-daily-life/finding-a-place-to-rent/), section "Know your rights as a renter"

- We say that in most cases federal law bans housing discrimination on the seven protected classes, and that in general a landlord has to go through the courts to make a tenant leave. We tell readers to call legal aid if they are locked out or utilities are cut. Anything wrong or missing?

## If you have more time

### 4. Aging, Death, and Wills

[anytimebook.org/big-life-decisions/aging-death-and-wills](https://anytimebook.org/big-life-decisions/aging-death-and-wills/)

- We say that without paperwork, state law usually picks a person's closest relatives to decide for them and to inherit. Many readers are estranged from those relatives. Is the page clear enough about why that matters?
- We say family members generally do not have to pay a dead relative's debts, with exceptions for co-signers, shared accounts, and some states' rules for spouses. Fair?
- The page points to a free online will tool. Is there a state where that is a bad idea that we should name?

### 5. Having Kids, or Not: legal exposure

[anytimebook.org/big-life-decisions/having-kids-or-not](https://anytimebook.org/big-life-decisions/having-kids-or-not/), section "If you are pregnant and not sure what to do"

- The page states no abortion law. It says laws differ by state and have changed, and links two nonprofit information lines. Does linking those create legal risk for a reader in a state with a ban? Is there a safer way to point people to accurate information?

### 6. Credit and Debt: identity misuse by a parent

[anytimebook.org/money/credit-and-debt](https://anytimebook.org/money/credit-and-debt/), section "If someone used your name or number"

- We say a credit freeze needs no police report, and suggest talking to legal aid before deciding whether to report a family member. Anything a reader should know before filing an identity theft report that names a parent?
