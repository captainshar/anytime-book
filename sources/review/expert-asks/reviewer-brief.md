# For reviewers: what we are asking, in one page

Thank you for considering this. Here is everything you need to decide.

## What the book is

*The Anytime Book* is a free, plain-language guide to everyday adult life in the United States. It is for adults starting from hard places: neglect, isolation, educational neglect, high-control groups, or a very different culture. Many readers have little money, no ID, and no one to ask. It lives at [anytimebook.org](https://anytimebook.org) and as a free ebook. Nothing in it is for sale, and it carries no ads.

## Why we need you

Some pages give safety, legal, or health information. A mistake on those pages could hurt someone. We have checked every phone number and program against its own official website, but checking sources is not the same as knowing the work. You know what happens in real life. We don't want to publish those pages as finished until someone like you has read them.

## How the book was made, honestly

AI tools did much of the research, checking, and drafting, and a person makes the choices about what goes in. The details are on the [How this book was made](https://anytimebook.org/how-this-book-was-made/) page. Early research got roughly one detail in four wrong, which is why every resource was rechecked on the organization's own site and why we are asking people in the field to read the risky parts. The record of what was checked, where, and when is public in the [project's repository](https://github.com/captainshar/anytime-book/tree/main/sources/resources).

## The ask

- **Time:** 30 to 60 minutes. Your sheet lists the exact pages and sections, most important first. Stop whenever you run out of time.
- **Read for harm, not style.** We want to know:
  1. Is anything **wrong**?
  2. Is anything **dangerous**, even if true? For example, advice that is safe for most people but could put someone at risk if the person hurting them found out.
  3. Is anything **missing** that a reader needs before they act?
  4. Is anything **stated too surely** when it depends on the state, the situation, or the person?
- **You are not giving advice to readers**, and we will never say you did. Every page tells readers it is information, not legal or medical advice.

## How to reply

Any way that is easy for you. A reply by email with "page, section, problem" is plenty. A marked-up printout or a 20-minute phone call works too. You do not need a GitHub account.

## Credit, your choice

Tell us which you prefer:

1. Your name and role on the page: "Reviewed by Jane Doe, legal aid attorney, November 2026."
2. Your role only: "Reviewed by a domestic violence advocate, November 2026."
3. No mention at all.

We will not use your name or your organization's name without your written yes. A review does not mean you or your employer endorse the book. If we change a page after your review in a way that touches what you checked, we will take the credit line down until it has been read again.
