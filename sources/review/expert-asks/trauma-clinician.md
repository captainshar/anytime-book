# Review sheet: trauma-informed therapist or counselor

Read [what we are asking](reviewer-brief.md) first. About 30 minutes.

Our readers often carry complex trauma from childhood, and many were taught to distrust therapy. Most cannot afford it. The book does not try to treat anyone. It offers a few free tools and tries not to make things worse. We would like you to tell us where it might.

### 1. Sleep

[anytimebook.org/first-things/sleep](https://anytimebook.org/first-things/sleep/)

- After a meditation app, the page adds a caution: if sitting still with your thoughts feels worse, stop, and that this is common after hard times. Is that the right thing to say, and enough?
- A tip says a night-light or an open door is fine if the dark feels unsafe. It is a judgment call with no source. Good advice?

### 2. Calming Your Body

[anytimebook.org/your-mind-and-feelings/calming-your-body](https://anytimebook.org/your-mind-and-feelings/calming-your-body/)

- Whole page. Could any exercise here make things worse for someone with a trauma history, such as breath work, body scans, or closing the eyes? Should any carry a caution or an alternative?

### 3. Naming What Happened

[anytimebook.org/your-mind-and-feelings/naming-what-happened](https://anytimebook.org/your-mind-and-feelings/naming-what-happened/)

- Whole page. Does it push readers toward labels or toward retelling before they are ready? Is the point at which it says "this is a time to get help now" in the right place?

### 4. Resting Without Guilt

[anytimebook.org/fun-rest-and-being-you/resting-without-guilt](https://anytimebook.org/fun-rest-and-being-you/resting-without-guilt/)

- For readers from groups that punished rest. Does anything here sound like the voice they left?

### 5. One question about the whole book

- Pick any other page that interests you. Where did the book talk down to the reader, alarm them, or promise a feeling it cannot deliver?
