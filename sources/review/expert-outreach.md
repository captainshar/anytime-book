# Finding expert reviewers: Sharon's kit

Written 2026-11-12. Some pages give safety, legal, tax, or health information that no script or AI pass can sign off on. This kit is how to get those pages read by people who do the work. The pages and the reasons are listed in [README.md](README.md), section 1.

Everything a reviewer needs is in [`expert-asks/`](expert-asks/): one [reviewer brief](expert-asks/reviewer-brief.md) that explains the ask, and one sheet per role with the exact pages and questions. The repo is public, so you can send a link to a sheet, or paste it into an email.

## How it works

1. **Pick a role** from the table below, most urgent first.
2. **Find two or three people** for it using the routes listed. Expect about one in three to say yes.
3. **Send the short email** below with the brief and that role's sheet. Add your own two sentences about why you are making this. That part matters most and only you can write it.
4. **When a reply comes in,** paste it into a Claude Code session and say "expert feedback for 9.2." Each point gets logged in the Area's review file, proposed as a page change, and shown to you for approval, the same way fact-check corrections are.
5. **Record the sign-off** only as the reviewer agreed (name and role, role only, or nothing).

Start now. This takes four to eight weeks and runs alongside everything else.

## Keep people's details out of this repo

**This repository is public.** Do not put reviewers' names, email addresses, employers, or who has and hasn't replied in any file here. Keep your tracking list somewhere private, like a note or a spreadsheet of your own. The only thing that goes in the repo is the credit line a reviewer has said yes to in writing.

A private tracker needs six columns: role, person, how you found them, date asked, reply, credit choice.

## The first email

Short on purpose. Change the bracketed parts.

> **Subject:** Would you read one page of a free guide for adults leaving isolation? (30 to 60 minutes)
>
> Hello [name],
>
> I'm Sharon Campbell. I'm making a free, plain-language guide called *The Anytime Book* for adults who are starting everyday life from hard places: neglect, isolation, high-control groups. [Two sentences in your own words: why you are making it.]
>
> A few pages give [safety / legal / health / tax] information. I don't want to call them finished until someone who does this work has read them. Would you, or someone on your team, read [the one page / the few sections] that touch your field? I'd like to know what's wrong, dangerous, or missing. It takes 30 to 60 minutes.
>
> Here is exactly what I'm asking, and the pages with my specific questions:
>
> - What I'm asking: https://github.com/captainshar/anytime-book/blob/main/sources/review/expert-asks/reviewer-brief.md
> - Your pages and questions: https://github.com/captainshar/anytime-book/blob/main/sources/review/expert-asks/[sheet].md
>
> I used AI tools heavily to research and draft the book, and I say so openly on the site. That is a big part of why I want a person with real experience to read these pages. You can be credited by name, by role only, or not at all, and I won't use your name or your organization's without your written yes.
>
> [If you can offer something: I can offer $__ as a thank-you for your time.]
>
> Thank you for considering it. A no is completely fine.
>
> Sharon
> anytimebook.org

Notes on sending:

- **Attach or paste the sheet** if you'd rather not send GitHub links. Some organizations' email filters dislike links from strangers.
- **Send from your own address,** one person at a time. No mass email. A named person at a local office says yes far more often than a general inbox at a national one.
- **Ask your own circle first.** A friend of a friend who is a nurse or a legal aid paralegal is the fastest yes.

## The follow-up (after about ten days, once)

> Hello [name], I wrote last week about reading a page of a free guide for adults leaving isolated or controlling homes. I know your time is short. If it's not something you can take on, could you point me to someone who might? Thank you either way. Sharon

After one follow-up, move on to the next person.

## The thank-you and credit check (after their review)

> Thank you. This is exactly what I needed. Here is what I changed because of your review: [two or three lines].
>
> Last question: how would you like to be credited? (1) Name and role, (2) role only, like "Reviewed by a domestic violence advocate, November 2026," or (3) not at all.

Send the list of changes even if they chose no credit. People who see their feedback used say yes the next time.

## What to offer

A thank-you payment is not required, and many advocates and educators review public materials as part of their jobs. For independent professionals and for lived-experience readers, paying something is the right thing if you can. A gift card or a modest flat amount for an hour is a common gesture; choose what you can sustain across fifteen or so reviewers. Some employers do not allow staff to accept payment, so offer it as optional.

## When a review comes back

- Every point is logged in the Area's review file under an "Expert review" heading, with the role and date, not the person's name (unless they chose name credit).
- Changes to a page go through the normal path: proposed, approved by you, applied, checked, merged.
- Where an expert and an official source disagree, the page says the more cautious thing and the review file records both.
- A page that has had its expert read can move from `draft` to `verified` once its fact-check log is also clean.
- **Showing the credit on the page** is a small follow-up to build: a frontmatter line such as `expert_reviewed: "a domestic violence advocate, 2026-11"` displayed in the page footer next to the fact-check and human-read dates. If a later edit touches what the expert checked, the line comes off until it is read again.
