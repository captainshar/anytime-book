### 6.1 Starting Your Medical Life

- **Vaccines.** The page lists no vaccine schedule, on purpose. It tells readers to ask their clinic, because advice "changed in 2025 and 2026." Please confirm you're comfortable with that framing.
- **"It is safe to get a shot again"** paraphrases CDC: "it is safe to repeat vaccines." That CDC page was last updated June 2024. Under the current ACIP, CDC wording may change.
- **Health care sharing ministries.** The page says they "are not insurance and don't have to pay your bills." That is accurate in general, but it is a values-adjacent warning for readers from religious backgrounds. Please check the tone.

### 6.2 Doctor, Dentist, and Eyes

- **EyeCare America** does **not** cover a glasses prescription (refraction) or glasses. It also excludes people with private insurance, an HMO or PPO, or VA benefits. Gemini left both limits out. The page now says this clearly. That leaves a gap: a free *glasses prescription* (see Hand to Gemini).
- **New Eyes fee.** The official page says there is a non-refundable fee but doesn't give the amount. Third-party listings say $15 since April 2025, and Gemini said $10. The page just says "small application fee."
- **RAM** is labeled "many states, but not everywhere." The October 2026 schedule showed about 17 states.

### 6.3 Is This an Emergency?

- **The 911 / ER / urgent care / wait list** is general triage information based on MedlinePlus. Please have a clinician read it.
- **ID wording.** The page says, "You don't need money, insurance, or ID to be seen for an emergency." That is my plain-words reading of EMTALA's "regardless of ability to pay." EMTALA says nothing about ID directly.
- **First-aid steps.** Gemini's detailed steps (choking, burns, spine injury, seizures) were left off as too risky to publish unchecked. Topic 10.4 may be a better home, using Red Cross material.

### 6.4 Medicine and Prescriptions

- **Safety advice.** "Don't stop a medicine suddenly, especially one for mental health, without asking your prescriber or pharmacist first" is general safety advice. Please confirm the wording.
- **For-profit services.** GoodRx and Cost Plus Drugs are for-profit companies. They are included because they are free to use or low-cost, and there's no equal public option for cash prices. Please confirm that fits the book's values.

### 6.5 Health Insurance and Benefits (big update)

- **Medicaid work rules.** These come from the CMS interim final rule fact sheet (June 1, 2026):
  - 80 hours a month, *or* earnings of 80 × the federal minimum wage (about $580 a month)
  - States must start by January 1, 2027, and may start earlier
  - 30 days to show you qualify after a notice
  - The list of who is excused
  - The page says "former foster youth under 26." The fact sheet just says "former foster youth"; "under 26" comes from the law. Please confirm.
  - CMS now calls H.R. 1 the "Working Families Tax Cut" law. The page doesn't name the law at all.
- **6-month Medicaid renewals.** These start with renewals on or after January 1, 2027, under Section 71107 and CMS letter SMDL #26-001. I saw this only in search summaries of medicaid.gov files, not the full text.
- **SNAP: official sources disagree.**
  - The public FNA page, fna.usda.gov/snap/work-requirements (updated July 16, 2026), **still lists the OLD exceptions**: homeless, veteran, and foster youth. It notes that guidance is pending.
  - The official guidance memo, issued October 3, 2025, removes those exceptions, raises the age to 64, and limits the child exception to children under 14. I couldn't download the memo PDF, so I confirmed these points from search summaries of fns/usda.gov pages.
  - The page follows the memo. Please confirm.
  - USDA's food agency now appears as the "Food and Nutrition Administration" (fna.usda.gov).
- **Expedited SNAP.** The rules ($150 / $100 / 7 days) come from the text of 7 CFR 273.2(i), read on Cornell LII because ecfr.gov blocked automated access. Please spot-check one state's page.
- **Marketplace.**
  - Open enrollment is November 1 to January 15 (from HealthCare.gov). The page says not to count on $0 plans.
  - I could **not** find a HealthCare.gov page that says in plain words that the enhanced subsidies expired. CMS documents say they "expire at the end of 2025 absent Congressional action." Please confirm nothing changed in 2026.
  - The year-round signup for people at or under 150% of the poverty line is not mentioned. The page just says "outside that time you need a life change."
- **Privacy on a parent's or spouse's plan (also in 6.6).** This comes from HHS's HIPAA Privacy Rule summary: plans "must accommodate reasonable requests" if disclosure "could endanger the individual," and they "may not question" that statement. The page says "ask in writing"; plans *may* require writing. "Some states protect you even more" is true in general, but I did not check which states.
- **Short-term plans.** The warning (may not cover pre-existing conditions, mental health, or pregnancy) is general knowledge, not checked against a 2026 federal rule. Short-term plan rules changed in 2024 and 2025.
- **FSA wording.** "Money you don't use by the deadline is usually lost, though some jobs let you keep a small amount or give you extra time." The glossary gives either a 2.5-month grace period or a carryover of up to $640.

### 6.6 Sexual and Reproductive Health (values calls)

- **Abortion.** The page lists AbortionFinder, the Repro Legal Helpline, and the M+A Hotline (which helps with self-managed abortion). It gives no state-by-state legality. Please confirm the whole section fits the book's values.
- **Crisis pregnancy centers.** The page says many "are set up to steer people away from abortion and may not share all your options." That is a values-laden warning. Please check the tone.
- **Scarleteen** is aimed at young people. It's included for adults who never got sex education.
- **Exams.** The page says, "You can ask for another staff member to be in the room... ask to stop at any time." This is worded as something you can *ask* for, not a legal right.
- **Funding changes.** Title X and Planned Parenthood funding changed in 2025 under H.R. 1 and Title X grant actions. Both finders still worked on October 4, 2026. The page tells readers to call ahead because listings may be out of date.
- **Emergency contraception.** Weight limits are left out. The page tells readers to ask a pharmacist.

### 6.7 Alcohol, Drugs, and Other Risks

- **Harm reduction.** Never Use Alone and NEXT Distro are included. That may be a values call for some readers.
- **Overdose signs.** "Won't wake up, very slow breathing, blue or gray lips" is general overdose information. Please have a clinician check it.
- **Twelve-step groups.** The paragraph on AA ("talk about a 'Higher Power'... you get to choose") is gentle on purpose. Please check the tone.
- **Part 2 privacy.** HHS says OCR issued guidance in September 2026 on state Medicaid agencies using substance-use treatment records to check exemptions from the work rules. You may want a line about this in 6.5 or 6.7. I didn't add one.
- **LifeRing's** site doesn't say "free" outright. The page says, "The site lists no fee to attend."

## Hand to Gemini

Paste Block A first, then one of these.

**1. Free or low-cost glasses prescriptions (refraction) for adults (6.2)**

> Topic: Getting an eyeglasses prescription for free or cheap as an adult with no insurance. EyeCare America does not cover refraction or glasses, so focus on how an uninsured adult gets a *glasses prescription that includes pupillary distance*. Include optometry school clinics (and how to find accredited ones), community health centers with vision services, Lions Club and other charity programs that pay for an exam, Medicaid adult vision coverage (and how to check your state's), and retail options with honest prices. Note which give the PD measurement. Flag ID and income rules.

**2. Free nurse advice lines and after-hours help for people without insurance (6.3)**

> Topic: Free nurse advice lines in the United States. Find which nurse advice lines are open to people *without* insurance — state or county public lines, Medicaid nurse lines, health center after-hours lines, and hospital system lines that are free to the public. Explain how to find whether one exists in your state or county. Note hours, whether you must be a member or patient, and languages. Do not include paid telehealth apps unless no free option exists.

**3. Medicaid work rules: how each state takes reports, and help with appeals (6.5)**

> Topic: The new Medicaid "community engagement" (work) rules starting by January 1, 2027, and 6-month renewals. Explain, using only official CMS, Medicaid.gov, and state Medicaid agency sources, how people report hours, what proof is accepted, how to claim an exemption (disability, caregiver, treatment, former foster youth), and what to do after a non-compliance notice. Explain how to find your own state's rules and start date (not a state-by-state table). Include free help: state Medicaid ombudsman or consumer assistance programs, legal aid, and health center enrollment staff. Note anything that changed after June 2026.

**4. Medical bills and hospital charity care (6.3 / 6.5)**

> Topic: Getting help with medical bills you already have. Find free, trustworthy help applying for nonprofit hospital financial assistance ("charity care"), reading an itemized bill, disputing errors, and handling medical debt in collections — including the No Surprises Act and CFPB rules on medical debt as of 2026. Include nonprofits that help for free (for example, ones that apply for charity care on a patient's behalf) and state consumer assistance programs. Warn about medical credit cards and debt-settlement scams.

**5. Insurance privacy for adults on a parent's or spouse's plan (6.5 / 6.6)**

> Topic: Keeping your health care private when you're an adult on a parent's or spouse's insurance. Explain HIPAA confidential-communications requests (45 CFR 164.522(b)) in plain language, how to make one with a health plan (sample wording), and which states have extra laws that send Explanation of Benefits (EOB) letters only to the patient or let patients choose. Explain how to find out if your state has such a law (not a full table). Include how to pay for sensitive care without using insurance (Title X, sliding-scale clinics). Official and legal-aid sources only.

**6. Drug checking supplies and harm reduction near you (6.7)**

> Topic: Free harm reduction supplies in 2026. Find where adults can get free naloxone and fentanyl/xylazine test strips by mail or in person nationwide, and how to find local programs and vending machines. Explain in plain words whether test strips are legal to carry in most states and how to check your own state (no state table). Confirm current status of NEXT Distro, harmreduction.org's finder, and Never Use Alone. Note any 2025–26 federal funding changes.

**7. Secular and women-centered recovery groups, current status (6.7)**

> Topic: Non-religious and alternative recovery support groups active in 2026. Confirm current status, cost, and meeting formats for SMART Recovery, LifeRing, Women for Sobriety, Moderation Management, Recovery Dharma, and Secular AA–style groups. Note which require abstinence, which allow moderation, and which are friendly to people with religious trauma. Official org sites only.
