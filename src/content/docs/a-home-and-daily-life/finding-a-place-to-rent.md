## What it is

Applying for a rental, reading a lease, turning on utilities, and knowing your rights as a renter.

## Why it helps you

A home of your own is a base for everything else. Knowing the rules keeps you from being taken advantage of.

## Good to have first

A Bank Account, Credit and Debt.

## This opens up

A stable place to build the rest of your life.

## Where to get help

### Get free advice before you sign

**[HUD-approved housing counselors](https://answers.hud.gov/housingcounseling/)**
Trained counselors help you plan what rent you can afford, read a lease, and spot bad deals. They help renters, not just home buyers.

- **Cost:** Free or low cost. Ask about fees when you call.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone.
- **How to start:** Call [1-800-569-4287](tel:18005694287) and ask for a housing counselor near you. You can also search by ZIP code at the [CFPB counselor finder](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/find-a-housing-counselor/).
- **Good to know:** The phone line has help in many languages. TTY: 202-708-1455. You can say, "I'm renting for the first time and I have no credit. Can you help me?"

**[SettleIn housing guides](https://www.settleinus.org/life-us/housing/newcomer%E2%80%99s-guide-renting-us)**
Simple guides to how renting works in the US: leases, deposits, and monthly bills. It is made for people new to the country, but it helps anyone new to renting.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone. Written for newcomers to the US.
- **How to start:** Open the guide and read the "Housing" section. Use the chat button if you want to ask a person.
- **Good to know:** Run by the International Rescue Committee. Chat help is offered in many languages. There is also a free app.

### Know your rights as a renter

Renter laws are different in each state, and sometimes each city. These tools point you to the rules where you live.

**[LawHelp.org](https://www.lawhelp.org/)**
Free, plain guides on renter rights, plus a list of free legal aid offices in your state.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone can read the guides. The legal aid offices mostly help people with low incomes.
- **How to start:** Pick your state from the list or map. Look for "Housing" or "Landlord and tenant."
- **Good to know:** You need internet. Local legal aid offices can also tell you about tenant groups in your area.

**[Find free legal aid near you](https://www.lsc.gov/about-lsc/what-legal-aid/i-need-legal-help)**
A map of free legal aid offices paid for by the Legal Services Corporation. These lawyers help with evictions, unsafe homes, and deposits.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** People with low incomes. Each office sets its own rules.
- **How to start:** Type in your address or city. Call the office that comes up.
- **Good to know:** If you get an eviction paper or a court date, call right away. Don't wait.

**[Report housing discrimination to HUD](https://www.hud.gov/reporthousingdiscrimination)**
In most cases, it is against federal law to refuse to rent to you because of your race, color, national origin, religion, sex, disability, or because you have children.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone who was treated unfairly when trying to rent.
- **How to start:** Call [1-800-669-9777](tel:18006699777) or report online. Say what happened, when, and who was involved.
- **Good to know:** There are time limits, so report soon. It is also illegal for a landlord to punish you for reporting.

### If a background check turns you down

**[CFPB: Review your rental background check](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/tenant-background-checks/review-your-rental-background-check/)**
Landlords often buy a report about you. These reports can have mistakes, like someone else's record mixed with yours. This page shows you how to get it fixed.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone turned down, or charged more, because of a background check.
- **How to start:** If a landlord turns you down because of a report, they must tell you which company made it. Ask that company for a free copy within 60 days. If something is wrong, tell the company in writing.
- **Good to know:** If they won't fix it, you can file a complaint with the CFPB at [1-855-411-2372](tel:18554112372).

### Help with bills and money to move in

**[Help with heating and cooling bills (LIHEAP)](https://liheapch.acf.gov/referral.htm)**
LIHEAP is a government program that can pay part of your energy bill. This free line tells you where to apply in your area.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** People with low incomes. Each state sets its own rules.
- **How to start:** Call [1-866-674-6327](tel:18666746327). Or email energyassistance@ncat.org with your city, county, and state.
- **Good to know:** When you apply, you will likely need ID and proof of income.

**[211](https://www.211.org/)**
A free, private phone line that connects you to local help, like rent help, deposit help, and utility help.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone.
- **How to start:** Dial 2-1-1. You can say, "I'm trying to move into my first apartment. Is there help with a deposit or first month's rent?"
- **Good to know:** 211 is run by local centers, so hours and programs depend on where you live.

## If you only do one thing first

Before you pay any fee or sign anything, call a free housing counselor at [1-800-569-4287](tel:18005694287). If you have no credit or no rental history, that is common, and it is not a dead end. A counselor can help you plan a budget and find landlords who will work with you. They can also tell you about renter classes near you. Some people bring proof of income, a letter from an employer or caseworker, or a co-signer (someone who agrees to pay if you can't).

## Watch out for

- **Paying for a place you haven't seen.** Scammers copy real listings and say they are "out of the country." Never pay or sign until you have been inside the home and met the landlord.
- **Wire transfers, gift cards, or crypto.** A real landlord will not ask for a deposit this way. Once that money is sent, you can't get it back. Report scams at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/).
- **Rent that is much lower than other places nearby.** A deal that seems too good, plus pressure to decide today, is a classic scam. Search the address online. If it shows up "for sale" or under another name, walk away.
- **Giving your Social Security number too early.** Scammers ask for it before you've seen the place. Share it only with a real landlord after you've toured and are ready to apply.
- **Paying lots of application fees.** Fees are usually not refunded. Before you pay, ask the landlord what they need to approve you, like income or credit.
- **Promises that aren't in the lease.** If the landlord says "I'll fix that," ask them to write it into the lease before you sign. On move-in day, take photos of any damage and send them to the landlord. This helps you get your deposit back.
- **Being locked out or having your power cut off.** In general, a landlord has to go through the courts to make you leave. If you are locked out or your utilities are cut off, call legal aid right away.
