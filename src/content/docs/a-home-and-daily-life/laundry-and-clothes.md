## What it is

Washing and drying clothes, sorting them, and small fixes like a loose button.

## Why it helps you

Clean, cared-for clothes last longer and help you feel ready for the world.

## This opens up

Doing Well at Work, Making Friends.

## Where to get help

### Learn how to wash and care for clothes

**[Laundry Basics (American Cleaning Institute)](https://www.cleaninginstitute.org/cleaning-tips/clothes/laundry-basics)**
Clear steps for sorting clothes, getting out stains, and reading the little symbols on clothing tags.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone.
- **How to start:** Read the part on sorting first. Keep the care-label symbol guide on your phone for laundry day.
- **Good to know:** This group is run by companies that make cleaning products. The tips are solid, but you don't need to buy any special brand.

### Free laundry

**[Laundry Love](https://laundrylove.org/find-a-location/)**
At set times, volunteers at local laundromats pay for the machines and give out soap, so you can wash and dry for free.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** People who can't easily pay for laundry. No forms are mentioned.
- **How to start:** Type your city or ZIP code into the map. Click a location to see the day and time. Bring your clothes during that time.
- **Good to know:** Each location sets its own load limit. Many are run by churches. Laundry Love doesn't list any religious rules for getting help. If anyone pressures you, you can say no or leave.

### Free clothes

**[211](https://www.211.org/)**
A free, private phone line that can point you to clothing closets, coat drives, and shoe programs near you.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone.
- **How to start:** Dial 2-1-1. You can say, "I need free clothes. Is there a clothing closet near me? What do I need to bring?"
- **Good to know:** Some closets ask for ID or proof of address, or let you come only once a month or so. Ask before you go.

**[Freecycle](https://www.freecycle.org/)**
Local groups where neighbors give things away for free, including clothes, coats, and shoes.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone with an email address.
- **How to start:** Find your town, make a free account, and look at "Offer" posts. You can also post a "Wanted" note with the sizes you need.
- **Good to know:** Pick things up in a public place or bring a friend if you can.

### Clothes for job interviews and work

**[Dress for Success](https://dressforsuccess.org/)**
Gives women free outfits for a job interview. Many locations also give work clothes once you're hired, plus help with job skills.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Women looking for work. Only in cities with a local office.
- **How to start:** Find your nearest location on the site. Call or email them and ask, "Do I need a referral, or can I sign up myself?"
- **Good to know:** Many locations need a referral from a partner group, like a job center, shelter, or caseworker. If you work with any of these, ask them to refer you.

## If you only do one thing first

Read the care tag inside one piece of clothing you wear a lot. If you're not sure what to do, wash it in cold water. Cold water is safe for most clothes and won't shrink them or make colors run. Wash whites apart from dark colors. Don't fill the machine more than about three-quarters full, so the clothes can move.

## Watch out for

- **Debit card holds at laundromats.** Some machines put a hold of extra money on your card, which can take days to clear. If money is tight, pay with coins or a laundry card you load with cash.
- **Putting stained clothes in the dryer.** Heat can make a stain permanent. Check for stains after washing. If you see one, treat it and wash again.
- **Laundry pods near kids.** They look like candy and are poison. Keep them up high and closed. If one is swallowed, call Poison Help at [1-800-222-1222](tel:18002221222).
- **Bleach with other products.** Never mix bleach with ammonia, vinegar, or other cleaners. It makes a toxic gas.
- **"Free clothes" offers that want your card number.** Online surveys or texts offering free clothes or gift cards for a "shipping fee" are scams. Real charities don't ask for your card.
- **Forgetting the lint trap.** Clean the dryer's lint screen before every load. A full one wastes money and can start a fire.
