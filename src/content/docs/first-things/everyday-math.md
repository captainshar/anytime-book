## What it is

The math of daily life — adding, subtracting, percentages, tips, measuring, and counting change.

## Why it helps you

It keeps you safe with money and lets you follow recipes, schedules, and bills.

## This opens up

Money, Cooking, Shopping Smart.

## Where to get help

### Practice on your own, at your own speed

**[Khan Academy — Arithmetic](https://www.khanacademy.org/math/arithmetic)**
Short videos and practice questions. They start with simple steps and build up to fractions and decimals. You get hints when you're stuck.

- **Cost:** Free. It is a nonprofit.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone.
- **How to start:** Go to the page. Click **Unit 1** and watch the first video. Go slow. Redo any part as many times as you like.
- **Good to know:** You can watch without an account. A free account (with an email) saves your progress. A big "donate" box may pop up. You can close it. Giving money is never required.

**[Math Antics](https://mathantics.com/)**
Friendly, clear videos that explain basic math with simple drawings. Good if you learn best by watching.

- **Cost:** The videos are free to watch.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone.
- **How to start:** Go to the website. Pick a topic like "Arithmetic" or "Fractions" and play the first video.
- **Good to know:** Some printable worksheets cost money. You don't need them.

### Practice with money

**[Math is Fun — Money Master](https://www.mathsisfun.com/money/money-master.html)**
A practice game where you count bills and coins and make change. You can try it many times, alone, before doing it at a real store.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone.
- **How to start:** Open the page. Pick **US Dollars**. Drag money on and off the counter.
- **Good to know:** No account needed. The site has ads. Don't click them.

### Find a free class or tutor near you

**[National Literacy Directory](https://www.nld.org/)**
A free phone line and website that find adult classes near you, including math.

- **Cost:** Free to call or search. Many classes are free or low-cost. Ask before you sign up.
- **Who can use it:** Any adult in the US.
- **How to start:** Call [1-877-389-6874](tel:18773896874). You can say, "I'm an adult. I want help with basic math. My zip code is ___."
- **Good to know:** No ID needed to call. The line is open 24 hours a day.

:::tip[Your phone has a calculator]
Most phones have a free calculator app. Using it is not cheating. Adults use calculators every day — at work, at the store, and paying bills.
:::

## If you only do one thing first

Open Money Master and practice making change for a few minutes. Try paying for a $3.50 item with a $5 bill, and count back what you'd get. Small, real practice like this builds confidence fast. Mistakes here cost nothing.

## Watch out for

- **Fake diplomas.** Websites that sell a high school diploma or "instant GED" are scams. Real ones need a real test. See the [FTC's warning signs](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/high-school-diploma-scams).
- **Fake checks for "tutoring."** A stranger sends you a check for too much money. Then they ask you to send some back. The check will bounce, and you will owe the bank. Never send money back from a check a stranger gave you. The [FTC explains fake check scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-spot-avoid-and-report-fake-check-scams).
- **"Pay us a share of your future paycheck."** Some schools offer deals where you pay them part of your pay for years. These can be costly and hard to get out of. Start with free adult classes first.
- **Subscriptions that are hard to cancel.** Some homework and learning apps make quitting very hard. Before you give a card number, find out how to cancel. The tools on this page don't need one.
