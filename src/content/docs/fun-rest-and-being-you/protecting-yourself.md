This page covers two things. One is what to do in the minutes after someone is badly hurt. The other is how to protect yourself from a person who means harm. If you were raised to handle everything inside the family or the group, calling for outside help may be new. Here it's the first step, and anyone is allowed to take it.

:::tip[Start here]
Save two numbers in your phone, or write them on paper by the door: **911**, and **Poison Help, [1-800-222-1222](tel:18002221222)**. Then read "If someone is badly hurt" below once. It takes two minutes.
:::

:::caution[In an emergency]
If someone is badly hurt, not breathing, or in danger, call [911](tel:911) first.
:::

## How it works

### If someone is badly hurt: check, call, care

These are the American Red Cross's three first aid steps.

1. **Check.** Make sure the place is safe for you. Then check the person: shout, tap their shoulder, and shout again. Look for breathing and for heavy bleeding. The check is quick: no more than 10 seconds.
2. **Call 911** if the person doesn't respond, isn't fully awake, isn't breathing or is only gasping, or is bleeding badly. If other people are there, point at one and say, "You, call 911."
3. **Care** for the person until help comes. The next sections say how.

If you are alone with someone who won't respond, MedlinePlus says to call 911 first, and then begin CPR.

### What to say when you call 911

Say where you are first, then what happened. For example: "I'm at 410 Oak Street, apartment 3. A man collapsed and he isn't breathing."

If you don't know the address, say what you can see: a street sign, a store name, a mile marker. Then answer the questions you're asked, and stay on the phone until you're told you can hang up.

[Is This an Emergency?](/health-and-your-body/is-this-an-emergency/) covers when to call 911 and when another kind of care fits.

### If the person is not breathing: Hands-Only CPR

Hands-Only CPR is pushing on the chest without breathing into the mouth. The Red Cross teaches it for adults, for people who aren't trained in full CPR. Babies and children need different steps, which a class teaches.

1. Call 911, or have someone else call.
2. Kneel beside the person.
3. Put the heel of one hand on the center of their chest. The heel is the firm part of your palm, near the wrist. Put your other hand on top.
4. Lean over so your shoulders are above your hands.
5. Push hard and fast: at least 2 inches down, 100 to 120 pushes a minute. That is about two pushes every second.
6. Let the chest come back up after each push.

An AED is a machine, kept in many public places, that checks the heart and gives it a shock if one is needed. If there is one nearby, send someone to get it and follow its instructions exactly.

Do not start chest pushes on someone who is breathing normally, coughing, or moving.

### If the person is bleeding badly

These steps are from MedlinePlus, the National Library of Medicine's health site.

1. Press directly on the wound with a clean cloth, a bandage, or a piece of clothing. If you have nothing, use your hand.
2. Keep pressing. Don't lift the cloth to look.
3. If blood soaks through, leave the cloth there and put another one on top.
4. Don't pull out anything that is stuck in the body, like a knife or a stick.

### If it might be poison

If someone swallowed, breathed in, or touched something that might be poison, call Poison Help at [1-800-222-1222](tel:18002221222). It's free and open 24 hours a day. It doesn't have to be an emergency. They can tell you whether you need 911.

If the person has collapsed or can't breathe, call 911 instead.

### What not to do

- **Don't move someone** who may have hurt their head, neck, or back.
- **Don't give food or drink** to someone who is unconscious.
- **Don't leave them alone.** Keep them warm until help arrives.

### What to keep at home

MedlinePlus lists these for a basic first aid kit:

- adhesive bandages
- sterile gauze pads and adhesive tape
- antiseptic wipes
- disposable gloves
- scissors
- a first aid guide

Most of it is sold at a pharmacy or supermarket. Keep it all in one place. MedlinePlus has a [longer list](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/001958.htm) if you want to add to it.

### What self-defense is

Most self-defense isn't fighting. The National Women's Martial Arts Federation (NWMAF) certifies teachers of a kind of class called empowerment self-defense. It defines self-defense as "anything we think, say, or do that helps us feel safe, strong, and respected."

A good class teaches four things:

- noticing what's going on around you
- speaking up firmly
- using your voice to stop someone
- simple physical moves for getting away

NWMAF says the moves are ones that "people of different ages, sizes, and abilities can do."

By NWMAF's standards, a good class also:

- lets you decide how much you take part, with no pressure to do any exercise
- covers people you know, like dates and partners, as well as strangers
- respects whatever a person did to get through a bad situation, and doesn't blame them
- uses consent throughout the class, including for touch

:::note[If someone you live with or depend on is hurting you]
A class isn't the first step. An advocate can help you plan for your safety, for free. Call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at [1-800-799-7233](tel:18007997233), or see [Leaving Family Safely](/big-life-decisions/leaving-family-safely/).
:::

### If you froze when something happened

Freezing doesn't mean you can't protect yourself, and it doesn't make what happened your fault. NWMAF puts it this way: whatever someone does or doesn't do in the moment, "they aren't at fault. The responsibility lies 100% with the aggressor."

Empowerment self-defense classes teach how bodies react under threat, and ways to manage the rush of adrenaline. [Calming Your Body](/your-mind-and-feelings/calming-your-body/) covers a body that stays on alert afterward.

## Where to get help

### Learn first aid for free

**[First aid steps (American Red Cross)](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/first-aid/performing-first-aid/first-aid-steps)**
The check, call, care steps on one page, made to be printed and kept on your refrigerator or in your bag.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone with internet. No sign-up.
- **How to start:** Open the page and print it. A library can print it for you.
- **Good to know:** The page also advertises paid classes and supplies. You don't need them to use the steps.

**[Hands-Only CPR (American Red Cross)](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/cpr/performing-cpr/hands-only-cpr)**
The CPR steps above, on a page you can print.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone with internet
- **How to start:** Read the steps once, then print the page and keep it with your first aid supplies.
- **Good to know:** Want to watch it instead? The [American Heart Association's Hands-Only CPR page](https://cpr.heart.org/en/cpr-courses-and-kits/hands-only-cpr) has short free videos, also in Spanish and Mandarin.

**[Stop the Bleed online course](https://www.stopthebleed.org/get-trained/online-course/)**
A free online course from the American College of Surgeons. It teaches three ways to stop heavy bleeding: pressing on a wound, packing a wound, and using a tourniquet. A tourniquet is a tight band around an arm or leg.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone with internet
- **How to start:** Open the link and start the course. You can take it as many times as you like.
- **Good to know:** The site also lists in-person classes, which usually last 90 minutes or less. Most are free. If none is near you, the site says to ask your local hospital to hold one.

**[First Aid app (American Red Cross)](https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/mobile-apps.html)**
A free phone app with step-by-step help for burns, bleeding, bites, allergic reactions, CPR, and more. It can read the steps out loud, so your hands stay free.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone with an Apple or Android phone
- **How to start:** In your app store, search "American Red Cross" and pick the first aid app. Download it now, before you need it.
- **Good to know:** It also helps you find the nearest hospital. It works in English and Spanish.

**[Poison Help](https://poisonhelp.hrsa.gov/)**
Poison experts on the phone, for when someone swallowed, breathed in, or touched something that might be poison.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone in the US
- **How to start:** Call [1-800-222-1222](tel:18002221222).
- **Good to know:** Open 24/7, with help in more than 100 languages. You can call with a question when nothing is wrong yet.

MedlinePlus has plain guides to many more injuries, like burns, stings, and sprains, on its [First Aid page](https://medlineplus.gov/firstaid.html).

### Get a CPR and first aid card for a job

**[American Red Cross classes](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/first-aid)**
Some jobs, like child care or security, need a CPR and first aid card. The Red Cross offers classes in person, online, or a mix of both.

- **Cost:** The online-only adult First Aid/CPR/AED course is $37. In-person prices change by city. Check the class listing for the price before you pay.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone
- **How to start:** Type your city or ZIP code in the class finder. Choose "Classroom Only" or "Blended Learning" if you need the card for a job.
- **Good to know:** The online-only course **does not** meet workplace (OSHA) rules. Before you pay, ask your boss which card they accept and whether they pay for the class.

### Find a self-defense class

**[Empowerment Self-Defense instructors (NWMAF)](https://nwmaf.org/empowerment/search-nwmaf-certified-instructors/)**
A list of teachers certified by the National Women's Martial Arts Federation. NWMAF says its teachers teach with awareness of how trauma affects people.

- **Cost:** Set by each instructor
- **Who can use it:** Mostly women, LGBTQ+ people, and non-binary people
- **How to start:** Find a certified instructor near you on the list. Email or call and ask: "Do you have a beginner class? What does it cost? Who can come?"
- **Good to know:** The list is short. If no one on it is near you, call [211](tel:211) or ask at your library whether anyone nearby runs a self-defense workshop.

**[R.A.D. Systems program locator](https://www.rad-systems.com/program_locator_student.html)**
R.A.D. trains instructors who teach its self-defense courses around the country. It has courses for women, for men, and for seniors.

- **Cost:** Set by each host. The R.A.D. site doesn't list prices, so ask.
- **Who can use it:** Depends on the course. The basic course is for women only. "R.A.D. for Men" is a separate course.
- **How to start:** Pick your state from the locator. Contact a host and ask about the next class, the cost, and who teaches it.
- **Good to know:** Courses run nine hours or more. R.A.D. says its instructors teach at colleges, universities, and police departments, and at other community groups. If you'd rather not take a class from police, ask who hosts it before you sign up. After you finish a course, you can come back to R.A.D. classes to practice for free.

## Watch out for

- **"Instant" CPR cards online.** A site that sells a CPR card in 10 minutes may be selling something your job won't accept. Ask your boss which card they take, then sign up through the Red Cross or the American Heart Association directly.
- **Fear-based sales pitches.** Be wary of a teacher who tells frightening stories and then pushes you to buy gear, sprays, or a long program. A class is there to add to what you can do, not to what you're afraid of.
- **Long contracts on day one.** Some martial arts gyms push year-long contracts on your first visit. You never have to sign that day. Look for one-time classes or workshops instead.
- **Classes that ignore your "no."** You always get to say "stop," take a break, or sit out. If a teacher touches you without asking, mocks your fear, or won't let you step away, leave.
