Rest is anything that lets your body and mind recover. Sleep is one kind. So is sitting, play, quiet, and time when nobody needs anything from you. If you grew up valued for your work, stopping can bring on guilt, or even panic. This page explains why, what counts as rest, and how to practice it until it gets easier.

:::tip[Start here]
Set a timer for five minutes and do one restful thing: sit, lie down, look out a window, or listen to a song. If guilt shows up, let it be there and keep going until the timer ends. If sitting still feels bad, walk slowly for the five minutes instead.
:::

:::caution[If you are thinking about harming yourself]
Call or text [988](tel:988). It's free and open any time.
:::

## What's going on

### Why stopping feels wrong

If resting got you punished, shamed, or called lazy, you learned that stopping is dangerous. If your worth was counted in chores, service hours, or how much you did for others, you learned that stopping makes you worth less.

So when you sit down now, something like an alarm goes off inside you. It can be guilt, restlessness, or a sudden list of things that have to be done this minute. That alarm is a habit you were trained into. It isn't a fact about you, and it isn't laziness.

A habit like this usually loosens with practice. Each time you rest and nothing bad happens, the alarm has a little less to stand on. This is slow. Count it in weeks.

### What counts as rest

Rest is more than sleep. Different kinds fix different kinds of tired.

- **Sleep.** It has its own page: [Sleep](/first-things/sleep/).
- **Doing nothing.** Sitting, lying down, looking out a window.
- **Quiet things.** A book, a puzzle, a slow walk, a long shower, music.
- **Play.** Anything done for fun. See [Finding What You Enjoy](/fun-rest-and-being-you/finding-what-you-enjoy/).
- **Time off from people.** Being alone, or with someone who needs nothing from you.
- **Time off from noise.** The phone face down. The news off.

Does TV count? One test works for anything: ask how you feel afterward. If you feel more settled, it was rest. If you feel tense or numb, that's a sign to try something else next time.

You don't have to meditate, and nothing here has to be spiritual.

### If relaxing makes you feel worse

For some people, sitting still with nothing to do makes things louder. Thoughts race, or old memories come up. That doesn't mean rest isn't for you. It means stillness is the wrong kind of rest for now.

Rest with your eyes open and your body moving. Walk. Wash dishes slowly. Listen to music while you do something with your hands. If panic or memories keep coming, [Calming Your Body](/your-mind-and-feelings/calming-your-body/) has tools for that.

### Tired, burned out, or something for a doctor

Being tired after hard work, stress, or short sleep is normal. Being tired for weeks is different. MedlinePlus is the National Library of Medicine's health site. It says to contact a health care provider if you've felt tired for weeks. A provider is a doctor, a nurse practitioner, or a clinic. The same goes for tiredness that doesn't lift with enough sleep, food, and less stress.

Lasting tiredness can come from things a provider can test for. Examples are anemia (your blood isn't carrying enough oxygen), thyroid disease, sleep apnea (your breathing stops or gets shallow while you sleep), and depression.

MedlinePlus says to make an appointment if you're tired and also have any of these:

- a fever, regular sweats, or weight loss you can't explain
- waking up many times during the night
- frequent headaches
- feeling sad or depressed

Get help right away if you have confusion or dizziness, blurred vision, or thoughts of harming yourself.

**Burnout** is exhaustion from stress that goes on and on. Mental Health America says burnout is usually tied to one role, like a job or caring for someone. It can improve with rest or fewer demands. **Depression** affects all of life and doesn't lift after a break. The two look alike, and you may not be able to tell them apart on your own. A provider can.

Tell the provider everything on that list that fits, including feeling down. [Doctor, Dentist, and Eyes](/health-and-your-body/doctor-dentist-and-eyes/) covers finding low-cost care.

## Things to try

1. **Five minutes on a timer.** The Start here step. Do it daily for a week before you judge it.
2. **Put rest on the schedule.** "Rest, 2:00 to 2:20" is as real as an appointment. You don't have to tell anyone what the time is for.
3. **Mark the end of work.** Wash your hands, change your shirt, or say "done for today" out loud. It tells your body the shift is over, even if the list isn't finished.
4. **Do one thing without doing a second thing.** Drink your coffee or tea without your phone. Eat one meal sitting down.
5. **Drop one task that can wait.** Leave the laundry unfolded tonight. Notice that nothing happens.
6. **Go outside for a few minutes.** No errand attached.

### If you have no time

Two jobs, kids, or caring for someone can mean a free hour doesn't exist. Rest in the gaps instead. These come from Mental Health America's burnout page:

- Sit quietly for a minute or two before you get out of the car or off the bus.
- Unclench your jaw and drop your shoulders. Do it now.
- Take 15 minutes to eat before you answer anyone.
- If you have kids and there's an adult you trust, ask them to cover for 15 minutes.

### When someone says you're lazy

To someone else, you don't owe a reason. "I'm off right now. I'll get to it tomorrow" is a full answer. [Boundaries and Saying No](/your-mind-and-feelings/boundaries-and-saying-no/) has more words for this.

If the people around you count any rest as selfish, you can rest without announcing it. A walk, a shower, or a slow cup of tea doesn't need anyone's permission.

When the voice is in your own head, answer it plainly: "Tired is not lazy. I get to stop." If that voice is harsh most of the time, see [Being Kind to Yourself](/your-mind-and-feelings/being-kind-to-yourself/).

## Where to get help

### If you're tired all the time

**[Fatigue (MedlinePlus)](https://medlineplus.gov/fatigue.html)**
A plain page from the US National Library of Medicine on what causes lasting tiredness and when to get it checked.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone with internet. No sign-up.
- **How to start:** Read the top of the page. For a list of signs that mean "call a provider," open [this MedlinePlus article](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003088.htm) and go to "When to Contact a Medical Professional."
- **Good to know:** It lists many possible causes, some serious. Most tiredness has an ordinary cause. The list is there so you know what a provider will check.

### If you're worn down by work or caregiving

**[Burnout: signs, causes, and how to recover (Mental Health America)](https://mhanational.org/resources/burnout-signs-causes-recover/)**
A short page from a long-running mental health nonprofit. It has separate lists of small steps for workers, parents, caregivers, and students.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone with internet
- **How to start:** Find the list that matches your life. Pick one step and do it today.
- **Good to know:** It also explains how burnout is different from depression. You can print it as a checklist.

### If you want to read more

**[The 4Fs: A Trauma Typology (Pete Walker)](https://www.pete-walker.com/fourFs_TraumaTypologyComplexPTSD.htm)**
An article by a therapist who writes about growing up in hard homes. It explains why some people can't stop working ("flight") or can't stop pleasing others ("fawn").

- **Cost:** Free to read
- **Who can use it:** Anyone with internet
- **How to start:** Read the parts on "flight" and "fawn" first. Take breaks if it stirs things up.
- **Good to know:** It's long and uses some therapy terms. The site also sells his books. You don't need to buy anything to read the articles.

Two books, both in many public libraries:

- ***[Laziness Does Not Exist](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Laziness-Does-Not-Exist/Devon-Price/9781982140113)* by Devon Price.** A psychologist argues that what gets called "lazy" is usually tiredness, fear, or not knowing the first step.
- ***[Rest Is Resistance](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/titles/tricia-hersey/rest-is-resistance/9780316365536/)* by Tricia Hersey.** Says rest is a basic human right, not a reward. The author is a theologian and the book uses spiritual language. If that's not for you, skip this one.

To borrow them free on a phone, use **[Libby](https://libbyapp.com/)**, the library app. Download it and search for your library. No library card? Tap "I Would Like A Card," then "Use My Phone Number." Many US libraries give you a digital card that way. Popular books can have a waitlist.

## Watch out for

- **Paid "nervous system" coaches.** Some online coaches charge a lot to "fix" burnout or trauma and have no license. Start with the free tools here. If you want more help, look for a licensed professional.
- **Groups that sell peace.** If a group says rest only comes through buying their program, cutting off friends, or obeying a leader, leave. That is control, sold as wellness.
- **Pills and teas that "cure" exhaustion.** Tiredness is a symptom, not a disease, so no supplement cures it. If you've been tired for weeks, a provider visit will tell you more than a product will.
- **Quizzes that charge at the end.** Some sites ask personal questions and then want payment to show your results. Close the page.
- **Resting "after."** There is always one more thing to do. If rest waits until the list is empty, it never comes. Put it on the schedule first.
