Plenty of adults can't swim. U.S. Masters Swimming, a national adult swimming group, says about one-third of US adults can't safely swim the length of a pool. It's a skill, and adult beginner lessons exist to teach it. This page covers how to stay safe near water now, what to do if someone is in trouble, and how to find a lesson.

:::tip[Start here]
Call one pool near you and say: "Do you have beginner swim lessons for adults? What do they cost? Do you have financial help?" Until you can swim, wear a life jacket near water, stay where a lifeguard is on duty, and don't go in alone.
:::

## How it works

### Stay safe before you can swim

These rules come from the American Red Cross.

1. **Wear a life jacket.** The Red Cross rule for inexperienced swimmers is to wear one whenever you're in, on, or around water. That holds even if a lifeguard is there. On a boat, everyone wears one, however well they swim.
2. **Check the life jacket.** Look for a stamp that says it is U.S. Coast Guard approved. Check the label for the weight it's made for. Don't use one with torn fabric or loose straps.
3. **Go where there is a lifeguard,** and never swim alone.
4. **Don't drink alcohol** before or while you're in the water.
5. **Don't count on pool toys.** Water wings, swim rings, and inflatable toys are not a substitute for a life jacket.

Lakes, rivers, and the ocean are harder than a pool. The Red Cross warns about drop-offs where the water gets deep without warning, fast currents even in shallow water, and sudden changes in water temperature.

You don't owe anyone an explanation. "I'm not a swimmer, so I'm wearing this" is enough. So is "I'll stay on the dock."

### What an adult lesson is like

Red Cross adult lessons let you learn at your own pace. You master one set of skills before moving to the next, and a certified instructor teaches the class. The first level is called "Learning the Basics."

Each pool runs its own classes, so ask before you sign up. Good questions:

- "Where does the beginner class start? Is it in shallow water?"
- "What do I bring?" Most people bring a swimsuit and a towel. If you don't own a swimsuit, or want more cover, ask what the pool allows before you buy anything.
- "What swimwear is allowed? Can I wear a full-coverage swimsuit?" Pools set their own rules on this. If one pool says no, try another. The answer can be different a few miles away.
- "Do you have a women-only class or swim time?" Each pool decides this too.
- "Is there a private place to change?"

For a picture of a first lesson, read [one man's account](https://www.usms.org/fitness-and-training/articles-and-videos/articles/my-first-swim-lesson-surprises-and-success) of his, at age 47. He had been afraid of water all his life. He stood chest-deep and practiced moving water with his hands. He did breathing exercises. Then he put his face in a little at a time: chin, then mouth, then his whole head. His instructor gave him goggles, which helped. Your lesson may go differently.

If you're afraid of water, say so when you call and again at the first class. "I had a bad experience in water. I'd like to go slowly." If the fear shows up as panic in your body, [Calming Your Body](/your-mind-and-feelings/calming-your-body/) has tools you can practice on dry land first.

### How you'll know you can swim

The Red Cross calls it water competency. It means you can do these five things:

1. Get into water that's over your head and come back to the surface.
2. Float or tread water for at least one minute. Treading water means staying upright with your head above water by moving your arms and legs.
3. Turn over and turn around in the water.
4. Swim at least 25 yards (about 23 meters).
5. Get out of the water.

Until you can do all five, keep using the rules above.

### If someone is in trouble in the water

Drowning is usually fast and quiet. The Red Cross says to watch for a person who:

- is not moving forward
- is upright in the water but can't move or tread water
- is face down and still


1. **Shout for a lifeguard.** If there isn't one, have someone call 911.
2. **Reach or throw. Don't go.** Hold out something long, like a pole or a broom. Or throw something that floats: a life jacket, a cooler, a ball.
3. **Stay out of the water.** If you go in, you could need rescuing too.

[Protecting Yourself](/fun-rest-and-being-you/protecting-yourself/) covers what to do once the person is out.

### If you are the one in trouble

The Red Cross teaches these steps for anyone who gets too tired to keep swimming.

1. **Stay calm.** Panic makes it hard to think.
2. **Roll onto your back and float** if you know how, or tread water. Both use less energy than swimming.
3. **Grab anything nearby that floats,** or a safety line if there is one.
4. **Shout** to get the attention of a lifeguard or the person you came with.

Swim lessons teach floating and treading water. Until you can do them, this is what the life jacket and the lifeguard are for.

### If a rip current pulls you

A rip current is a channel of water flowing away from the beach. They happen at ocean beaches and on the Great Lakes. These steps are from the [National Weather Service](https://www.weather.gov/safety/ripcurrent-surviving).

1. **Relax.** A rip current pulls you away from shore. It doesn't pull you under.
2. **Don't swim straight toward shore.** You'd be fighting the current.
3. **Swim along the shoreline** until you're out of the pull. Then swim at an angle, away from the current, toward shore.
4. **If you can't reach shore,** relax, face the shore, and call or wave for help.

The Weather Service also says to swim only at beaches with lifeguards if you can, and never alone. Its rule: "If in doubt, don't go out."

## Where to get help

Swim lessons are run by local pools, so prices change from place to place. The groups below help you find a class. When you call, ask: "Do you offer financial assistance?"

### Find adult swim lessons

:::tip[Your city pool may be the cheapest]
City and county **Parks and Recreation** departments run parks and community centers, and often public pools with adult lessons and open swim times. Search for your city or county name plus "parks and recreation pool." Or walk in and ask at the front desk.
:::

**[YMCA: Find Your Y](https://www.ymca.org/find-your-y)**
The Y has pools and swim lessons in many towns. It teaches adults as well as kids.

- **Cost:** Set by each local Y. Many Ys offer financial help based on income.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone
- **How to start:** Type your ZIP code to find the nearest Y. Call or visit and say: "I'd like adult beginner swim lessons. I don't have much money. Do you have financial assistance?"
- **Good to know:** Financial help may ask for proof of income. If you don't have papers, explain your situation and ask what else they accept.

**[American Red Cross Adult Swim Lessons](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/swimming/swim-lessons/adult-swim-lessons)**
The Red Cross writes the lesson plans many pools use. Its page explains the three adult levels.

- **Cost:** Set by the local pool that teaches the class
- **Who can use it:** Older teens and adults at any level
- **How to start:** Go to the [Learn-to-Swim Providers map](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/swimming/learn-to-swim-providers) and pick a pool near you. Call and ask about adult classes and cost.
- **Good to know:** Lessons are taught by a certified Water Safety Instructor.

**[U.S. Masters Swimming: Adult Learn to Swim](https://www.usms.org/alts-central/learn-to-swim)**
A national adult swimming group that trains instructors to teach adults. Its page speaks to adults who never had the chance to learn, and to adults who had a frightening experience in water.

- **Cost:** Set by each instructor or club
- **Who can use it:** Adults who can't swim or want to start over
- **How to start:** Click "Find an Instructor" and search near you.
- **Good to know:** If you had a frightening experience in water, tell the instructor at the start.

### Stay safe in lakes, rivers, and the ocean

**[Sea Tow Foundation Life Jacket Loaner Program](https://www.boatingsafety.com/page/life-jacket-loaner-program)**
Free life jackets you can borrow at more than 1,500 stations. They are at boat ramps, marinas, and parks around the country.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone
- **How to start:** On the page, click "See the map here" to find a station near you. Take a life jacket and bring it back when you're done.
- **Good to know:** Not every lake or beach has a station. Check the map before you go.

The safety rules on this page come from the [American Red Cross water safety page](https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/types-of-emergencies/water-safety.html). If you look after children near water, that page has a free online course called "Water Safety for Parents & Caregivers."

## Watch out for

- **Teachers who push you.** A class that forces your face under, or moves you to deep water before you agree, is the wrong class. You can get out of the pool and leave. Look for a teacher who is certified, like by the Red Cross or the Y.
- **Going in deeper so you don't have to say you can't swim.** It's a hard thing to say in front of people. Have a line ready: "I'm staying where it's shallow."
- **Jumping in to save someone.** A person in trouble in the water can pull you under. Reach or throw from land, and call 911.
- **Long gym contracts.** Some private gyms have you sign a long contract to use the pool. Ask if you can pay one month or one class at a time.
