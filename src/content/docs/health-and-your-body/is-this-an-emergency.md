## What it is

Knowing when to wait, when to see a doctor, when to go to urgent care, and when to call 911.

## Why it helps you

It saves you money and fear, and it can save a life — yours or someone else’s.

## This opens up

Calm, confident choices in a scary moment.

## Where to get help

:::caution[This is information, not medical advice]
If you think someone could die or be badly hurt, call 911 now. You can sort out the cost later.
:::

### A simple guide

- **Call 911** if someone can't breathe, is choking, has severe chest pain or pressure, suddenly can't speak, see, walk, or move, is bleeding heavily, has passed out, or has a seizure. An ambulance team can start care on the way.
- **Go to a hospital emergency room (ER)** for serious problems when the person is steady enough to ride in a car. Examples: a deep cut, a bone that may be broken, or coughing or throwing up blood.
- **Go to urgent care** for a mild problem that needs care today. Examples: a sore throat, an earache, a sprain, or a small cut.
- **Wait for a regular visit** for things that are mild and not getting worse, like a refill or a rash that isn't spreading.

The government's health library, [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000593.htm), has a longer list of warning signs. If you have insurance or Medicaid, look on the back of your card for a **24-hour nurse line**. A nurse can help you decide where to go. Some health centers have an after-hours line for their patients, too.

### Help on the phone

**[988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline](https://988lifeline.org/)**
Free support any time you are in emotional pain, in crisis, worried about drugs or alcohol, or just need to talk.

- **Cost:** Free and confidential.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone in the US.
- **How to start:** Call or text **988**, or chat at 988lifeline.org.
- **Good to know:** Open 24/7. Text and chat are also offered in Spanish.

**[Poison Help](https://poisonhelp.hrsa.gov/)**
Poison experts tell you what to do if someone swallowed, breathed in, or touched something harmful, or took too much medicine.

- **Cost:** Free and confidential.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone in the US. You can call even if you're not sure it's an emergency.
- **How to start:** Call [1-800-222-1222](tel:18002221222). Have the bottle or package nearby.
- **Good to know:** Open 24/7, with interpreters in many languages. If the person is not breathing or won't wake up, call 911 first.

**[National Maternal Mental Health Hotline](https://mchb.hrsa.gov/programs-impact/national-maternal-mental-health-hotline)**
Counselors for people who are pregnant or just had a baby and feel overwhelmed, sad, or anxious.

- **Cost:** Free and confidential.
- **Who can use it:** Pregnant and new parents. Partners and family can call too.
- **How to start:** Call or text [1-833-852-6262](tel:18338526262) (1-833-TLC-MAMA).
- **Good to know:** Open 24/7 in English and Spanish.

**[211](https://www.211.org/)**
A free line that connects you to local help, like low-cost clinics, food, and housing.

- **Cost:** Free and confidential.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone.
- **How to start:** Dial **211** and say, "I need a low-cost clinic near me." Or search at 211.org.
- **Good to know:** Each area runs its own 211, so services differ by place.

### Your rights at the hospital

**[Emergency care rules (CMS)](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/regulations-guidance/legislation/emergency-medical-treatment-labor-act)**
A federal law called EMTALA says most hospital ERs must check you and treat an emergency, whether or not you can pay.

- **Cost:** You may still get a bill later.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone who comes to a hospital ER.
- **How to start:** Go to the ER. You don't need money, insurance, or ID to be seen for an emergency.
- **Good to know:** Most hospitals are nonprofits. The [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/financial-assistance-policies-faps) requires them to have a "financial assistance policy" for people who can't pay. Ask the billing office for the application, even after you get the bill.

## If you only do one thing first

Save these numbers in your phone today: **911**, **988**, and **Poison Help, 1-800-222-1222**. Then find a community health center near you (see [Starting Your Medical Life](/health-and-your-body/starting-your-medical-life/)). Having your own clinic means you'll have somewhere to call when you're not sure.

## Watch out for

- **ERs that look like urgent care.** Some emergency rooms stand alone in shopping areas and look like urgent care. They can bill like a hospital ER. If it isn't an emergency, ask at the door, "Is this urgent care or an emergency room?"
- **Skipping the ER because of cost.** In a true emergency, don't wait. The hospital must treat you first. Ask about financial assistance afterward.
- **Urgent care and money.** Urgent care clinics are not required to see you for free and often ask for payment first. Ask the price before you're seen.
- **Waiting out a mental health crisis alone.** Feeling like you might hurt yourself is an emergency, too. Call or text 988 any time.
