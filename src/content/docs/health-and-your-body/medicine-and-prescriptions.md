## What it is

How to fill a prescription, take medicine safely, and get refills — including for mental health.

## Why it helps you

The right medicine, taken right, helps you heal. There are programs that make it affordable.

## This opens up

Steady health.

## Where to get help

:::note[A few basics]
- A **prescription** is a doctor's or nurse's order for a medicine. They send it to a pharmacy, or give you a paper.
- The **pharmacist** is trained in medicines. You can ask them anything, for free: how to take it, side effects, or whether it mixes badly with other things you take.
- Ask for the **generic** version. It has the same active ingredient and usually costs much less.
- Don't stop a medicine suddenly, especially one for mental health, without asking your prescriber or pharmacist first. Some need to be stopped slowly.
:::

### Pay less at the pharmacy

**[NeedyMeds Drug Discount Card](https://www.needymeds.org/drug-discount-card)**
A free card from a nonprofit. Show it at the pharmacy to lower the cash price of many medicines.

- **Cost:** Free card. You still pay the discounted price.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone. No income rules.
- **How to start:** Get the free app, or print the card from the website. Show it to the pharmacist before you pay.
- **Good to know:** You can't use it together with insurance or Medicaid. You can ask the pharmacist which price is lower and pick one. NeedyMeds also lists free-medicine programs from drug companies. The helpline is [1-800-503-6897](tel:18005036897) (Mon–Fri, 9 a.m.–5 p.m. Eastern).

**[GoodRx](https://www.goodrx.com/)**
A free website and app that compares cash prices at nearby pharmacies and gives you a coupon.

- **Cost:** Free to search and use coupons. You don't need the paid membership.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone.
- **How to start:** Search your medicine's name, pick the pharmacy with the lowest price, and show the coupon there.
- **Good to know:** GoodRx is not insurance. Prices can be very different from one pharmacy to the next, so compare.

### Medicine by mail

**[Rx Outreach](https://www.rxoutreach.org/faq/)**
A nonprofit mail-order pharmacy with low cash prices on many common medicines.

- **Cost:** Low cost.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone with a valid prescription. There's no income test or sign-up application.
- **How to start:** Call [1-888-796-1234](tel:18887961234) (Mon–Thu 7 a.m.–6 p.m., Fri 7 a.m.–5:30 p.m. Central), or start online. Your prescriber can send the prescription to them.
- **Good to know:** You need a mailing address (a PO box is okay). They take debit or credit cards, checks, and money orders. They don't bill insurance.

**[Mark Cuban Cost Plus Drugs](https://www.costplusdrugs.com/)**
An online pharmacy that shows exactly how each price is made: its cost, a markup, and a fee.

- **Cost:** Low cost on many generics. Check the price on the website first.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone with a valid prescription.
- **How to start:** Search your medicine, make a free account, and ask your prescriber to send the prescription there.
- **Good to know:** You need a mailing address and a payment card. It takes only some insurance plans.

### Low-cost clinic pharmacies

**[Find a Health Center (HRSA)](https://findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov/)**
Community health centers charge based on income. Many have their own low-cost pharmacy or a partner pharmacy.

- **Cost:** Based on your income.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone.
- **How to start:** Become a patient. Ask, "Do you have a pharmacy program for people without insurance?"
- **Good to know:** Staff can also help you apply to a drug company's free-medicine program. These programs often need a doctor's signature and proof of income.

### Buy online safely

**[Safe Site Search (NABP)](https://safe.pharmacy/buy-safely/)**
The group that supports state pharmacy boards checks online pharmacies. Type in a website address to see if it is safe.

- **Cost:** Free.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone.
- **How to start:** Paste the pharmacy's web address into the search box.
- **Good to know:** NABP says nearly 95% of websites selling prescription-only drugs operate illegally. A web address ending in **.pharmacy** has been checked by NABP. If a medicine looks wrong or makes you sick, you can tell the FDA at [MedWatch](https://www.fda.gov/safety/medwatch-fda-safety-information-and-adverse-event-reporting-program) or [1-888-463-6332](tel:18884636332).

## If you only do one thing first

Next time you get a prescription, ask two questions before you pay: "Is there a generic?" and "What is the cash price with a discount card?" Then compare with GoodRx or NeedyMeds. This one habit can save a lot of money.

## Watch out for

- **Online pharmacies that don't ask for a prescription.** A real pharmacy always needs one. Fake ones sell pills that may contain the wrong drug or deadly fentanyl. Check the site with NABP first.
- **Pills from friends or social media.** Pills that look real can be fake. Only take medicine from a licensed pharmacy.
- **Paying for a "free" discount card.** NeedyMeds and the basic GoodRx coupons are free. You never need to give bank details to get a basic discount card.
- **Paying someone to apply for free medicine.** Some websites charge to fill out drug company forms for you. Your clinic can help you find and fill them out for free.
- **Medicine that looks different.** If pills or packaging look strange, ask the pharmacist before you take them.
