## What it is

Using a computer and phone — typing, searching, saving files, and getting around the internet.

## Why it helps you

These skills are now required for jobs, school, and services. The good news: they’re very learnable.

## This opens up

Online safety, school, and most jobs.


## Where to get help

### Learn at your own pace, for free

**[DigitalLearn](https://www.digitallearn.org/)**
Short videos with a calm voice that walk you through using a computer, the internet, and email. Made by the Public Library Association. It works well on a phone.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone.
- **How to start:** Open the site and pick a beginner course, like computer basics or email basics.
- **Good to know:** You need internet. You do not need an account. You can make one if you want to save your progress. In English and Spanish.

**[Northstar Digital Literacy](https://www.digitalliteracyassessment.org/)**
Short online check-ups that show what computer skills you have and which ones to work on next. Run by Literacy Minnesota, a nonprofit.

- **Cost:** Free to try the check-ups online.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone.
- **How to start:** Open the site and choose a check-up, like "Basic Computer Skills" or "Email." You can do them without an account.
- **Good to know:** To earn a certificate you can show an employer, you take the test at a Northstar location, like some libraries or adult schools. Ask your library if it is one.

### Talk to a real person

**[Senior Planet Tech Hotline](https://seniorplanet.org/)**
A free phone line where patient helpers answer any tech question. "How do I set up email?" is a fine question. Run by Senior Planet from AARP.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Made for people 60 and older.
- **How to start:** Call [888-713-3495](tel:18887133495). Say what device you have and what you are trying to do.
- **Good to know:** Open Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 8 p.m. Eastern Time. You do not need to be an AARP member.

**[211](https://www.211.org/)**
211 is a free, private phone line that connects you to local help. Many areas have free computer classes, cheap internet plans, or low-cost computers. 211 can tell you what is near you.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone in the US.
- **How to start:** Dial 2-1-1 on any phone. Say, "I want free computer classes or help getting cheap internet or a computer near me."
- **Good to know:** What is offered depends on where you live. Low-cost computer programs often ask for a photo ID and proof of low income.

## If you only do one thing first

Go to your nearest public library and ask the front desk, "Can I use a computer today? I'm just learning." Many libraries give a guest pass if you don't have a library card or ID yet. Then open DigitalLearn and start the first lesson. Librarians help beginners every day. You will not break anything.

## Watch out for

- **Fake virus warnings.** A pop-up may say your computer is infected and tell you to call a number. It is fake. Don't call. Close the browser. If the screen is frozen, hold the power button until it turns off.
- **"Tech support" calls you didn't ask for.** Real companies don't call to say your computer has a problem. Hang up. Never let a stranger control your computer from far away.
- **Paying to learn the basics.** You don't need a paid "computer course" to learn email, typing, or the internet. The free tools above cover it.
- **Public computers forget everything.** When your library time ends, the computer often wipes your files. Save your work to your email or a cloud drive before time runs out. Always sign out of your email.
