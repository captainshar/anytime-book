## What it is

Strong passwords, privacy settings, spotting fake or misleading content, and keeping your accounts safe.

## Why it helps you

It protects your money, your identity, and your peace — and helps you keep family or others from tracking you.

## Good to have first

Computer and Internet Basics.

## This opens up

Safe use of everything online.


## Where to get help

### Learn the basics of keeping accounts safe

**[Secure Our World (CISA)](https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world)**
Plain tips from the US government's cybersecurity agency. It covers four habits: strong passwords, two-step login, updating your phone and computer, and spotting fake messages.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone.
- **How to start:** Open the page and start with "Use Strong Passwords."
- **Good to know:** No account needed.

**[Bitwarden password manager](https://bitwarden.com/pricing/)**
A password manager is an app that remembers all your passwords for you. You only remember one strong password. Bitwarden has a free plan.

- **Cost:** The free plan covers unlimited passwords on all your devices. A paid plan is about $20 a year, but you don't need it.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone.
- **How to start:** Make a free account. Pick a long main password that only you know, like four random words.
- **Good to know:** You need an email address. If you forget your main password, the company cannot get it back for you. Write it down and keep it somewhere safe.

### If someone may be watching or tracking you

**[Safety Net Project (techsafety.org)](https://www.techsafety.org/resources-survivors)**
Guides from the National Network to End Domestic Violence. They explain spying apps (often called stalkerware), location tracking, phone safety, and how to save proof of abuse.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone. Written for people facing abuse or stalking, including from family.
- **How to start:** Read "Technology Safety Plan" first. If you think your phone is being watched, read the guide on stalkerware.
- **Good to know:** Read these on a device the other person has never touched, like a library computer.

**[National Domestic Violence Hotline](https://www.thehotline.org/)**
Trained advocates who can help you make a safety plan, including for your phone and accounts. This is not only for partners. It can help if a parent or group is controlling you.

- **Cost:** Free and private
- **Who can use it:** Anyone.
- **How to start:** Call [1-800-799-7233](tel:18007997233). Or text START to 88788. Or chat on the website.
- **Good to know:** Open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

**[Safety Check for iPhone (Apple)](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/safety-check-iphone-ios-16-ips2aad835e1/web)**
A tool built into iPhones. It shows who can see your location, photos, and other info, and lets you stop sharing.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone with an iPhone on iOS 16 or newer.
- **How to start:** Go to Settings, then Privacy & Security, then scroll down to Safety Check.
- **Good to know:** "Emergency Reset" stops all sharing at once. The other person may notice. If you are not safe yet, talk to the Hotline before you change things.

### If you were scammed or someone used your identity

**[IdentityTheft.gov (FTC)](https://www.identitytheft.gov/)**
The Federal Trade Commission's official site for identity theft. It gives you a free, step-by-step recovery plan if someone opened accounts or used your name or Social Security number.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone.
- **How to start:** Go to IdentityTheft.gov and answer the questions. In Spanish, use RobodeIdentidad.gov. To report in another language, call [1-877-438-4338](tel:18774384338) and press 3.
- **Good to know:** Interpreters are there 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Eastern Time.

**[ReportFraud.ftc.gov (FTC)](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/)**
Where you report a scam to the FTC. You'll get next steps after you report.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone.
- **How to start:** Go to the site and click to report. Or call [1-877-382-4357](tel:18773824357).
- **Good to know:** The FTC does not get your money back for you. Call your bank or card company right away to try to stop or undo a payment.

### Tell what's true online

**[RumorGuard (News Literacy Project)](https://www.rumorguard.org/)**
Real viral posts, fact-checked, with simple steps to check claims yourself. Good practice if you grew up with only one source of "truth."

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone.
- **How to start:** Open the site and read one recent post. Notice the questions it asks about who made it and what proof it has.
- **Good to know:** No account needed.

## If you only do one thing first

Make your main email account safe. Your email is the key to almost every other account. Give it a new, long password that nobody else has ever known, including family. Then turn on two-step login in the email's security settings. If someone else ever had your password, also check which phones and computers are still signed in, and sign them out.

## Watch out for

- **Old passwords family may know.** Birthdays, pet names, and old passwords are easy for people who know you to guess. Change them, and change your security questions too.
- **"Private" browsing doesn't hide you from spy apps.** Incognito mode only keeps history off that device. If someone may have put an app on your phone, use a safer device, like a library computer.
- **Deleting proof too fast.** Before you block someone or delete scary messages, take screenshots. The Safety Net Project explains how. This proof can help later.
- **Callers who say they are the government.** The FTC and IRS will not threaten you or ask you to pay with gift cards, wire transfers, or crypto. Hang up.
- **People who offer to "hack back" an account.** These are almost always a second scam. Use the official "forgot password" or account recovery steps instead.
