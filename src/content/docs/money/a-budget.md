## What it is

A simple plan for the money coming in and going out.

## Why it helps you

A budget shows you where your money actually goes, so you get to decide where it goes next. It also warns you when a month is going to come up short, while there’s still time to do something about it.

## Good to have first

[Everyday Math](/first-things/everyday-math/), [A Bank Account](/money/a-bank-account/).

:::tip[Start here]
Print the [consumer.gov budget worksheet](https://consumer.gov/your-money/budget-worksheet). Fill in only the top part: the money that comes in each month. Next time, add the bills. All you need is a pencil and about half an hour.
:::

## How it works

1. **Write down what comes in.** Every paycheck, benefit, or gift you can count on in a month. If your pay changes, use a low month.
2. **Write down what you must pay.** Rent, utilities, phone, transportation, debt payments. Look at bills or bank statements so you’re not guessing.
3. **Track everything else for a few weeks.** Food, toiletries, small purchases. Keep receipts or write each one down. Most people are surprised by this list.
4. **Subtract.** Money in, minus money out. If it’s negative, you know before the month ends, not after.
5. **Decide on purpose.** Pay housing, utilities, and food first. Then choose what to cut, what to keep, and what to save.
6. **Check it once a month.** A budget is a draft. Change it when your life changes.

## Where to get help

### Start on paper

**[Budget Worksheet (consumer.gov)](https://consumer.gov/your-money/budget-worksheet)**
A one-page worksheet from the Federal Trade Commission (FTC), a government agency. You write down what money comes in. Then you write down what goes out. Then you subtract.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone
- **How to start:** Open the page and download the PDF. Print it, or copy the boxes onto any paper.
- **Good to know:** No account, email, or ID needed. A library can print it for you.

**["Behind on bills?" booklet (CFPB)](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_ymyg_behind-on-bills_print.pdf)**
A free booklet from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), a government agency. It is for months when there isn't enough money. It helps you choose which bills to pay first, like housing and utilities.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone
- **How to start:** Download the booklet. Start with the page that helps you list your bills and due dates.
- **Good to know:** You can print just the pages you need.

### Talk it through with a real person

**[National Foundation for Credit Counseling (NFCC)](https://www.nfcc.org/)**
A national network of nonprofit counselors. A counselor can help you build a budget that fits your real life. Helping people who are behind on bills is their whole job.

- **Cost:** The first talk is free. Some services, like a debt repayment plan, have a fee. Ask about fees before you agree to anything.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone in the U.S.
- **How to start:** Call [1-800-388-2227](tel:18003882227). Say: "I'd like help making a budget."
- **Good to know:** It helps to have a rough idea of your income and bills. You don't need papers to start.

**[GreenPath Financial Wellness](https://www.greenpath.com/counseling/)**
A nonprofit that is part of the NFCC network. Its counselors offer free, private sessions to look at your money and make a plan.

- **Cost:** Free counseling sessions. Some special services have a fee.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone in the U.S.
- **How to start:** Call [1-800-550-1961](tel:18005501961).
- **Good to know:** GreenPath says it does not lend money or do "debt settlement."

**[211](https://www.211.org/)**
A free, private phone line that connects you to local help. Ask about free money coaching near you, or help with food and bills.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone
- **How to start:** Dial 2-1-1 on any phone. Say: "I'm looking for free help making a budget near me."
- **Good to know:** What's available depends on where you live.

### Use an app (optional)

**[Goodbudget](https://goodbudget.com/)**
A budget app that uses "envelopes." You give each kind of spending, like food or rent, its own amount. You type in what you spend by hand.

- **Cost:** The free plan costs $0. It includes 20 envelopes, 1 account, and 2 devices. Premium costs $10 a month or $80 a year. You don't need it.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone with a phone or computer
- **How to start:** Sign up for the free plan. Make a few envelopes, like Rent, Food, and Phone.
- **Good to know:** You need an email address. The free plan does not connect to your bank.

## Watch out for

- **Apps that want your bank login.** Some free apps make money by studying your spending and showing you loan offers. Writing things down yourself keeps your information private.
- **"Free trials" that need a card.** If a budget tool asks for your card to try it, it may start charging you later. Set a reminder to cancel, or skip it.
- **Cash advance apps.** Apps that "advance" your pay often charge fees and ask for "tips." These add up fast. See [Money Traps to Avoid](/money/money-traps-to-avoid/) for more.
- **Paying someone to "fix" your money.** Real budget help is free from nonprofits like the ones above. Be careful of anyone who wants money first.
