## What it is

Comparing prices, reading reviews, handling returns, and watching subscriptions.

## Why it helps you

The same money stretches further, and you stop paying for things you don’t use.

## This opens up

A budget that goes further.

## Where to get help

### Shop safely

**[Online Shopping (FTC)](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/online-shopping)**
A plain guide from the Federal Trade Commission (FTC), a government agency. It covers comparing prices, checking a seller, and paying in a safer way.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone
- **How to start:** Read it before your next big online purchase.
- **Good to know:** Look at the total price, with shipping, fees, and tax. That's the real cost.

**[How to Evaluate Online Reviews (FTC)](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-evaluate-online-reviews)**
Tips for spotting fake or paid reviews.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone
- **How to start:** Next time you read reviews, try one tip. Check the dates. A burst of reviews in a short time is a warning sign.
- **Good to know:** The FTC suggests starting with trusted expert review sites, not just customer stars.

**[CamelCamelCamel](https://camelcamelcamel.com/)**
A free website that shows the price history of items on Amazon. You can see if a "sale" is really a lower price.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone
- **How to start:** Search for the item, or paste in its Amazon link. Look at the chart.
- **Good to know:** It only covers Amazon. You can set price alerts with a free account.

### Free trials, subscriptions, and cancelling

**[Free trials and auto-renewals (FTC)](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/getting-and-out-free-trials-auto-renewals-and-negative-option-subscriptions)**
Explains how "free" trials turn into monthly charges, and how to get out.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone
- **How to start:** Before a trial, write down the day it ends. Set a reminder a few days before.
- **Good to know:** If a company keeps charging you after you cancel, the FTC says you can dispute the charge with your card company. Keep proof that you cancelled.

**[The Cooling-Off Rule (FTC)](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/buyers-remorse-ftcs-cooling-rule-may-help)**
A federal rule for sales made at your home, workplace, or dorm, or at a seller's temporary spot, like a hotel room or fair. For many of these sales, you have three days to cancel.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone. It covers sales of $25 or more at your home. At temporary locations, it covers sales of $130 or more.
- **How to start:** Read the page to see what it covers. Cancel in writing within three days.
- **Good to know:** Some kinds of sales are not covered. The page lists them.

### If something goes wrong

**[ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/)**
Report a scam, a fake store, or a company that won't let you cancel.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone
- **How to start:** Click "Report Now," or call [1-877-382-4357](tel:18773824357).
- **Good to know:** First try the store. Write down what happened and what you want, like a refund.

**[State consumer protection offices (USA.gov)](https://www.usa.gov/state-consumer)**
Your state's office can take complaints about stores and may help settle a dispute. Some states also have their own rules that make cancelling a subscription easier.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone
- **How to start:** Choose your state, then follow the link to its complaint form.
- **Good to know:** Each state's office works a little differently.

## If you only do one thing first

Look at your bank statement for the last month. Circle any charge that repeats, like apps or streaming. Ask yourself, "Do I still use this?" Cancel one you don't need. That money is now yours again.

## Watch out for

- **Fake countdown timers and "only 1 left!"** These are made to rush you. You can take your time.
- **"Free" offers that need your card.** If it's really free, they shouldn't need your card. If they do, set a reminder to cancel.
- **Gift cards, wire transfers, or crypto as payment.** If a seller asks you to pay this way, it's a scam.
- **Prices that seem too good.** A big-name item at 80% off on an unknown site may be fake or never arrive. Search the store's name with "scam" or "complaint" first.
- **"Final sale" or "as is."** You usually can't return these. Check the return policy before you pay.
- **Overpaying sellers.** If you sell something and the buyer "accidentally" sends too much and asks for some back, it's a scam. The check or payment will bounce.
- **A federal "click to cancel" rule.** You may read about one. A federal court threw it out in 2025, so it isn't in force. Other laws still say companies can't trick you into staying.
