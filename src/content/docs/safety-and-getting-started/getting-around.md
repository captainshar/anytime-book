## What it is

Buses, trains, rides, and other ways to travel — including from far-out rural places.

## Why it helps you

You can’t reach a job, a clinic, or a friend you can’t get to. This is the key to the whole map.

## Good to have first

A Phone (helpful for maps and rides).

## This opens up

Work, Health, Friends.

## Where to get help

### Find the bus near you

Most towns and many rural areas have some public transit. "Transit" means buses, trains, or shared vans anyone can ride.

- **In a city or town,** buses stop at marked signs. Look up your local transit agency's website for maps and times. Map apps on a phone can also show bus routes.
- **In the country,** the "bus" may be a van you call a day or more ahead. Search for your county's name and the word "transit." Or ask 211.
- **Paying:** Many buses take cash, but the driver usually can't make change. Bring the exact amount. A regular ride usually doesn't need ID.
- **Cheaper fares:** Many transit agencies have lower fares for older adults, people with disabilities, and sometimes people with low income. Rules differ. Ask your transit agency about a "reduced fare." These programs often ask for ID.

### Help paying for rides

**[211 (United Way)](https://www.211.org/)**
A free phone line that can tell you who gives out bus passes, gas cards, or rides where you live.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone in the United States
- **How to start:** Dial [2-1-1](tel:211). Say, "I need help getting to work (or a doctor). Who gives out bus passes or rides near me?"
- **Good to know:** What's available depends on your area. Some programs run out of money, so ask more than one place.

**[Community Action Partnership: Find a CAP](https://communityactionpartnership.com/find-a-cap/)**
A map of Community Action Agencies. These local nonprofits help people with low income. Some help with transportation, like bus passes or rides.

- **Cost:** Free to search. Their help is usually free.
- **Who can use it:** People with low income. Each agency sets its own rules.
- **How to start:** Search by ZIP code, county, or state. Call the agency and ask, "Do you help with bus passes or rides?"
- **Good to know:** Services differ a lot from place to place. They may ask about your income.

### Rides to the doctor

**[Medicaid rides (Non-Emergency Medical Transportation)](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/medicaid-coordination/states/non-emergency-medical-transportation)**
If you have Medicaid, it may give you a free ride to and from medical visits. Medicaid is free or low-cost health coverage for people with low income. These rides are called NEMT.

- **Cost:** Free for people who qualify
- **Who can use it:** People on Medicaid who have no other way to get to a Medicaid-covered visit. For example, you don't have a working car or a driver's license.
- **How to start:** Call the number on your Medicaid card, your health plan, or your state Medicaid office. Ask, "How do I set up a ride to my appointment?"
- **Good to know:** Rules differ by state. You must set up the ride ahead of time. The ride can only take you to the medical visit and back home. Rides might be a bus pass, a van, a taxi, or a car.

**[American Cancer Society: Road To Recovery](https://www.cancer.org/support-programs-and-services/road-to-recovery.html)**
Free rides from volunteer drivers to cancer treatment.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** People going to a cancer-related medical visit
- **How to start:** Call [1-800-227-2345](tel:18002272345) and ask if rides are available where you live.
- **Good to know:** Not available everywhere. It can take several business days to set up a ride, so call well ahead.

**[DAV Transportation Network](https://www.dav.org/get-help-now/medical-transportation/)**
Free rides for veterans to VA medical centers, driven by volunteers.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Veterans going to VA medical care
- **How to start:** Find the Hospital Service Coordinator for your VA on DAV's website, or ask at your VA hospital.
- **Good to know:** Not every area has drivers. Plan ahead.

### A long trip to safety

**[National Runaway Safeline: Home Free](https://www.1800runaway.org/youth-teens/home-free)**
A free Greyhound bus ticket for young people to get to family or another safe place.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Ages 12 to 21 who are away from home, have no safe way back, and are ready to go to family or a safe adult who agrees to help. It can also be a safe program, like a youth housing program.
- **How to start:** Call [1-800-786-2929](tel:18007862929) (1-800-RUNAWAY). It's open 24/7.
- **Good to know:** A counselor talks with you first to see if you qualify.

If you are an adult leaving someone who hurts you, a domestic violence program may be able to help with travel. Call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at [1-800-799-7233](tel:18007997233), or text START to 88788. Ask about help getting to a shelter.

## If you only do one thing first

Dial 2-1-1 and say where you need to go. Ask who helps with bus passes, gas cards, or rides near you. If the trip is to a doctor and you have Medicaid, also call the number on your Medicaid card.

## Watch out for

- **Ride offers inside airports and stations.** Don't take a ride from someone who walks up and offers one. Use the official taxi line, or a ride app where you can match the license plate.
- **Fake QR codes.** Scammers stick fake codes on parking meters and bus stops. They lead to sites that steal card numbers. Pay with cash or with the transit agency's own app.
- **"Buy Here, Pay Here" car lots.** These lots sell cars on payment plans, often at very high interest. Some can shut off or take back the car fast if you miss a payment. Read every page before you sign. A credit union may be a safer place to ask about a car loan.
- **Using a Medicaid ride for errands.** The ride can only go to the medical visit and back. Using it for other stops can be treated as stealing from Medicaid.
