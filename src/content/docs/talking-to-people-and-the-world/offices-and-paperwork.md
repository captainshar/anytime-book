## What it is

Filling out forms, dealing with offices (like the DMV or benefits offices), and what to do when you’re told “no.”

## Why it helps you

Almost every Topic in this book passes through some office. Knowing how to handle them — and how to appeal — is real power.

## Good to have first

Phone Calls and Messages.

## This opens up

ID, Benefits, Housing, Taxes — nearly everything.

## Where to get help

### Find the right office

**[211](https://www.211.org/)**
A free helpline that knows the offices and programs near you. Tell them what you need, and they'll point you to the right place.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone
- **How to start:** Dial [211](tel:211) on any phone. Or search by ZIP code at 211.org. You can say: "I need help with a form and I don't know which office to go to."
- **Good to know:** Open 24/7. Private. Help in many languages.

**[USAGov](https://www.usa.gov/phone)**
The federal government's help line. They tell you which government agency handles your problem.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone
- **How to start:** Call [1-844-872-4681](tel:18448724681), or use the chat at usa.gov/chat.
- **Good to know:** Monday–Friday, 8 a.m.–8 p.m. Eastern time. Closed federal holidays.

**[Community Action agencies](https://communityactionpartnership.com/find-a-cap/)**
Local nonprofits that help people with low income get through programs, like help paying energy bills.

- **Cost:** Free to talk to them
- **Who can use it:** Mostly people with low or modest income. Rules differ by place.
- **How to start:** Use the finder. Enter your ZIP code to see the agency near you.
- **Good to know:** To get money help, you'll usually need ID and proof of income.

### Social Security

**[Social Security Administration](https://www.ssa.gov/manage-benefits/make-an-appointment)**
The federal office for Social Security cards, retirement, disability, and SSI (a monthly payment for people with low income who are disabled or 65+).

- **Cost:** Free. Never pay anyone for a Social Security form.
- **Who can use it:** Anyone with Social Security business
- **How to start:** Make an appointment online, or call [1-800-772-1213](tel:18007721213). Say: "I want to make an appointment." Your appointment may be by phone or in person.
- **Good to know:** Phone lines are open Monday–Friday, 8 a.m.–7 p.m. your local time. Waits are often shorter in the morning and later in the week. TTY: [1-800-325-0778](tel:18003250778). Offices now work mostly by appointment, but they say they won't turn away people who can't make one.

### Free legal help when the answer is "no"

**[LawHelp.org](https://www.lawhelp.org/)**
Plain-language guides, forms, and a list of free legal aid offices in your state.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Anyone can read the guides. Free lawyers are usually for people with low income.
- **How to start:** Pick your state from the map.
- **Good to know:** Each legal aid office has its own rules. Many ask for proof of income.

**[Legal Services Corporation (LSC)](https://www.lsc.gov/about-lsc/what-legal-aid/i-need-legal-help)**
The biggest funder of free civil legal aid. "Civil" means problems like benefits, housing, or family — not crimes.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** People with low income
- **How to start:** Type your city or address into the finder to see the closest legal aid office.
- **Good to know:** Call early. Appeal deadlines can be short.

**[ABA Free Legal Answers](https://abafreelegalanswers.org/)**
Type a legal question online, and a volunteer lawyer writes back.

- **Cost:** Free
- **Who can use it:** Adults with low income and a civil (not criminal) question. Not every state takes part.
- **How to start:** Answer a few questions to see if you qualify. Then make an account and type your question.
- **Good to know:** You need an email address. Answers come in writing, not right away.

:::note[If you're told "no"]
A "no" letter is often not the end. Most programs let you **appeal**, which means asking them to look again.

- **Read the deadline on your letter.** Each program has its own. For example, Social Security gives you 60 days to ask them to reconsider. Other programs may give you more or less time.
- Ask the office: "How do I appeal, and what is my deadline?"
- Keep a copy of everything you send.
- If you're not sure, call legal aid right away. They can help you with the appeal.
:::

:::tip[Staying organized]
- Keep your important papers (birth certificate, Social Security card, letters) in one folder, in a bag you keep with you.
- Take a phone photo of the front and back of each one.
- Keep a notebook. For each call or visit, write the date, the worker's name, and what they said.
- At an office, it's okay to say: "I'm new to this. Can you help me fill this out?" Public libraries are also a good place to ask for help with forms.
:::

## If you only do one thing first

Get one folder for your papers and one notebook for your calls. Then dial 211 and ask which office handles the thing you need. One step, one office at a time.

## Watch out for

- **Paying for free forms.** Some websites look official and charge $20–$200 for a blank government form. Real government forms are free. Look for a web address that ends in **.gov**.
- **"Expedited" document services.** Private companies charge extra to send the same form you could send yourself. Go straight to the official office.
- **"We guarantee you'll win."** No honest helper guarantees a win in a benefits, eviction, or immigration case. Real legal aid does not ask for big fees up front.
- **Calls that threaten arrest.** Social Security, the IRS, and police don't call to demand money. They never take gift cards or crypto. Hang up.
